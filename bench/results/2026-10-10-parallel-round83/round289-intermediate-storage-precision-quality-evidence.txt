# R289 — quality evidence for lower-precision intermediate storage

Date: 2026-10-10
Scope: literature and read-only source review. No implementation, build, test, GPU run, profiler, service action, Git operation, or cleanup. The question is specifically whether FP32 GEMM accumulation may be followed by FP16/BF16/other reduced-precision storage of intermediate activations or recurrent working values with an acceptable quality cost. Weight-only quantization and KV-cache quantization do not answer this question.

## Decision from available evidence

No published result found is an apples-to-apples, quantified demonstration that the current Strata Qwen3.8-Flash-Next buffers in `prefill.cpp` can be stored in FP16 or BF16 with near-zero degradation. The most directly related evidence is the Qwen Team's own technical report: it says storing the widened Gated Residual state in FP8 cuts traffic in half versus BF16 “with almost no loss in quality,” but gives no numeric quality delta, metric table, confidence interval, context length, or clearly specified inference benchmark for this storage change. That is evidence worth following up, but it is not enough to qualify a near-zero claim or to infer FP16/BF16 behavior for Strata's different working buffers.

The NVFP4 GDN paper is a useful related result, but its method is different: it quantizes weights and GEMM inputs blockwise to 4-bit, then continues arithmetic in high precision. It demonstrates robustness of that paper's Qwen3.8-27B recurrence and reports numerical quality results, but does not store Strata's intermediate MoE/GDN arrays in low precision. SmoothQuant is also a mismatch: it evaluates calibrated W8A8 linear operations after an equivalent scaling transform, not unscaled intermediate-output storage. Therefore neither is a published quality receipt for this proposal. Since the requested precision/operation/model match is absent, the next valid decision is a local paired benchmark before any adoption.

## Primary-source evidence and its limits

1. **Qwen Team model report, closest architecture match.** The Qwen3.8-Next report describes the target model and its Gated Residual (GR), GDN and QSA architecture. In its inference-efficiency section, it says the authors tried storing residual branches in FP8 because the gates bound written values; the format halves residual-state traffic relative to BF16 “with almost no loss in quality.” The same section reports that sparse residual writes looked nearly unaffected on pretraining metrics but degraded clearly after post-training, which is a useful warning that a training-loss check alone is insufficient. However, the report provides no numeric quality delta or evaluation protocol specifically for the FP8 residual-storage experiment. This is persistent, cross-layer GR state, not MoE `Dm`/`GU` output rows or GDN convolution/gate workspaces. Its claim is qualitative and cannot establish “near-zero” for any D buffer. Source: [Qwen Team, *On the Design of Qwen3.8-Next Architecture*](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30320v1), §2.2 inference efficiency.

2. **NVFP4 GDN study, quantized input/weight values but different model.** Kozyrev and Maiboroda evaluate Qwen3.8-27B, which has 48 GDN and 16 full-attention layers, not the 125B Flash-Next model. Their Minima variant applies NVFP4 W4A4 to 496 linear layers, including GDN projections; it excludes convolutions and norms. The paper evaluates at one RTX PRO 6000, vLLM 0.27.1, with FP8 KV and a fixed harness. Its headline table reports BF16 vs Minima WikiText-2 perplexity 6.95 vs 7.67 at 4K and 10.35 vs 10.84 at 32K; MMLU-Pro 80.4 vs 79.7, GSM8K 95.5 vs 95.5, AIME'25 86.7 vs 86.7, GPQA-D 86.5 vs 85.1, and LiveCodeBench v6 79.0 vs 78.5. The five-task average is 85.62 vs 85.10 (−0.52 points); all benchmark comparisons are reported within seed noise and RULER retrieval is 100 at 32K/64K for both. This is good evidence that this specific block-scaled W4A4 method is robust on the dense 27B GDN architecture. But the activation operands are fake-quantized for GEMMs and calculations continue in high precision; the persistent GDN state in the mechanism study is explicitly lockstep FP32. This is not low-precision storage of D's intermediate arrays, and does not support unscaled FP16/BF16 stores. Source: [Kozyrev & Maiboroda, arXiv:2609.04098](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04098v1), §§3–5, especially Table 1 and §5.3.

3. **SmoothQuant, activation quantization but a different operation.** SmoothQuant reports negligible loss for calibrated INT8 W8A8 on several model families. It moves activation outlier difficulty into weights using a mathematically equivalent per-channel scaling transform, then quantizes operands at GEMM time. That is not a cast of each FP32 GEMM result to FP16/BF16 before the next operation, and the paper does not establish quality for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, its GR paths, or per-token GDN state updates. Source: [Xiao et al., *SmoothQuant*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/xiao23c.html).

The cited papers' quality outcomes should not be used as degradation estimates for the proposed casts: all three differ in at least operation, format, model, or measurement completeness. In particular, identical checkpoint hashes are not an appropriate acceptance condition after intentional precision reduction; compare numerical distributions and task/log-probability behavior instead.

| GDN recurrent state | `ss.gdn_state` is addressed as `float*` at each GDN layer (`gdn_floats` includes the matrix state and convolution history); `gdn_recurrence` updates it through all prompt tokens. | Storing the state low precision rounds on repeated recurrent writes and is a separate, higher-risk experiment than low-precision per-layer scratch. FP16 and BF16 are not interchangeable. FP16 keeps more significand bits but has a finite maximum magnitude of 65,504; BF16 has FP32-like exponent range but fewer significand bits. For each candidate, collect pre-cast max/percentiles, non-finite count, saturation/underflow count and post-cast relative/absolute error by layer and token position. Do not use a “usually small activations” argument to waive overflow checks, particularly for unnormalized MoE rows or GDN gates/state.

## Minimum local quality benchmark if storage precision is pursued

Use the same weights, prompt tokens, tokenizer/template, routing, sampler, execution mode, chunking, and source as the current accepted control. The one changed variable is the selected storage dtype/buffer boundary. This must be a paired quality comparison, not a build-only check or a hash comparison. Keep FP32 accumulation enabled in both arms where the operation supports it.

1. **First establish numerical locality.** Use deterministic teacher-forced inputs and save, for the first diverging layer/token and each candidate tensor, reference/candidate max absolute and relative error, RMS error, cosine similarity, NaN/Inf/saturation counts, and layer/token index. For GDN, separately compare the projected inputs, gates, recurrent state and output. For `Dm`, compare expert contribution before route weighting, combined token output, then next-layer hidden state. Preserve current 12K/32K chunk boundaries and the existing long-context position path. These measurements show where rounding enters; they do not alone show model quality.

2. **Measure distributional language-model change.** On held-out, fixed-token text, teacher-force both arms and report mean next-token NLL delta in nats/token, per-document deltas and paired confidence intervals; report mean and tail KL divergence between next-token distributions, top-1 agreement, and token-level log-probability error. Include short and long windows (e.g. 4K, 32K, then the target full 256K context) and report position-binned values so repeated GDN-state rounding can be distinguished from per-layer scratch rounding. A large number of tokens is not a substitute for independent documents/sequences when estimating variance.

3. **Check task behavior that loss can miss.** Run deterministic code-generation/unit-test tasks, exact retrieval and multi-key retrieval at 32K and near 256K, plus held-out instruction/format/tool-call cases relevant to Strata. Use several fixed examples and repeated seeds only where the metric is stochastic. Compare with the baseline's own run-to-run variance; set the acceptable margin before looking at candidate results. Report individual outcomes, not just an average. A near-zero perplexity/NLL change alone does not establish unchanged code, retrieval, or long-context ability.

4. **Separate formats and scopes.** First try FP16 and BF16 as independent arms for one buffer family at a time (GU, Dm, GDN temporary input/output, or recurrent state). Capture underflow/overflow separately. Test combinations only after individual effects are understood. For recurrent state, include long sequence and state-restore tests; for chunk scratch, compare chunked prefill and whole-prompt control to rule out boundary-specific effects. A storage optimization that changes route sums or final logits intentionally will not be byte-identical; retain the original outputs, exact source/config hashes, sample-level metrics, fault outcomes, and a concise comparison report per `docs/ARTIFACT_RETENTION.md`.

No local benchmark was run in this task. Existing records show the current 12K chunk-major main output has a strict difference in 63 tensor parts with maximum absolute difference `0.8735669` and first output difference at index 2; that is a different experiment, not a measured storage-precision quality delta, and it does not establish an ability loss or tolerance for any cast. Existing D storage evidence and the cited literature should remain separate from that outstanding numerical gate.

## Recommended conclusion

Treat the Qwen authors' FP8 GR-storage statement as qualitative architecture-specific support for testing low-precision *residual-state storage*. It is not a quantified near-zero degradation result, and it does not justify changing MoE `GU`/`Dm` or GDN recurrent state. The NVFP4 GDN paper provides quantified, architecture-adjacent evidence for carefully scaled W4A4 activation/weight quantization on Qwen3.8-27B, but not for output-buffer storage. SmoothQuant is further away. For the current Strata proposal, require a local paired, teacher-forced and task-level benchmark for each storage boundary and dtype before calling quality loss near-zero or choosing a candidate.
