No build, test, GPU, profiler, or performance measurement was performed in this audit.

Finding
XeStrata contains plausible implementation candidates in prompt XMX/GEMM, fused GDN, QSA prompt attention, MoE dispatch, and AVX2 CPU expert kernels. Its source offers candidate ideas, not evidence of speed on the B570. The measured host is Arc Pro B70 (Xe2, 32 GB) with Core i7-14700; our target is RTX B570 10 GB with a six-core Ryzen 5 5600X. XeStrata specifically chooses Xe2 XMX, other reported matrix engines through joint_matrix, and DP4a otherwise. Thus its Xe2 XMX implementation does not apply on NVIDIA; its portable joint_matrix or CUDA-target builds may apply only if the device/compiler path and reported capabilities select them. No candidate can be promoted without a build and same-model, same-context, same-host paired measurements on the B570.

The project docs say XeStrata is derived from Strata 0.1.24, partly incorporates upstream through 0.1.39/0.1.40.2, and omits upstream SYCL as a separate competing implementation. Its prompt CPU-sharing path is an upstream-derived feature and may be independently useful. The Xe-specific XMX/QSA/GDN kernels have Xe-oriented layouts and hardware assumptions and require adaptation rather than direct reuse. XeStrata's own runtime contract requires device/host USM, subgroup width 32, FP16, and selects by reported capabilities; unsupported GPU paths are rejected. The documented B70 small-device checks are simulated B70 limits (8 GiB, max allocation 4 GiB, no XMX), not proof of operation on a 10-GB B570.

B70 record limits
`bench/results/2026-10-09-upstream-0140/README.md` states the test machine (B70 32 GB, RTX 4070 12 GB PCIe x4, i7-14700, 990 Pro), the model (IQ2_XS with stock draft, int8 KV), alternating arms, and workloads. Its prompt/decode end-to-end data use roughly 4,945-token fresh documents, 256 generated tokens, and short chats; other entries are medians of back-to-back microkernel launches at listed token/shape counts. Those results establish implementation-specific behavior on those cards and shapes, not a speedup estimate for B570/5600X. The README itself identifies regressions: MMQ prefill was 0.34x the default XMX FP16 route on B70; fused MoE 0.25x; GDN key-head sharing 0.54x; several AVX2 K-quant or prefetch changes were within noise. Keep the useful rejection evidence alongside any adopted positive candidate.

Candidate-by-candidate transfer screen

1. Prompt GEMM / XMX: `src/prefill/gemm.cpp`, `src/kernels/xe/xmx_gemm.cpp`, and `src/kernels/xe/mma_gemm.cpp`; grouped quantized expert path in `src/prefill/prefill.cpp` around lines 1890–1934, 2072–2129. The B70 XMX path is not a B570 kernel. Candidate to adapt is the abstraction/dispatch idea: use device-reported matrix combinations and a tested fallback, then compare the current SYCL/oneMath route against a B570 Tensor Core route. Do not transplant Xe DPAS tiles or Xe capability thresholds. Source is `/home/yayoi/.local/state/strata-sycl/post-reboot-tuning-20261007/actual-prefill-census-capacity-join-v1.json`, SHA-256 `b0afc60f795f47ea699281717d312cbd944b5d35fd25a0dff7949b7e14fbffb3`.

2. GDN: `src/kernels/xe/fused_gdn.cpp`; inspect the fused projection/norm/scan pipeline and event/barrier boundaries as design candidates. Xe record's one-token/microbatch gdn_ab_multi changed 5.28/8.87/12.43 to 3.98/5.80/8.53 us on B70 (T=2/4/6), and 6.2–6.7/10.6–10.7/15.0 to 5.89/9.64/14.01 us on RTX 4070. Its prefetch schedule was deliberately device-selected: six-to-four barriers regressed B70 (11.0→17.1 us) but helped RTX 4070 (9.16→8.55 us). This is the strongest indication that code-path structure may transfer to NVIDIA while exact tuning does not. Audit graph/event dependencies and temporary aliasing against Strata's queue model before porting.

3. QSA prompt attention: `src/kernels/xe/qsa_prompt_attn.cpp`. Xe source fuses grouped attention and online softmax and uses half inputs with float accumulators; its workgroup, subgroup, and matrix tile design is explicitly tied to Xe layouts. A B570 port would be a new backend implementation (or a generic algorithm extraction), not a direct XMX reuse. Measure attention-only and end-to-end at matching context, and check the current implementation's actual head/format route before assuming QSA addresses this workload. The upstream record shows attention-score work-item variants can be markedly worse at T=4 on both cards (the README lists e.g. B70 54.3/68.8→70.3/298.8 us and RTX 4070 36.2/89.5→72.2/198.0 us for T=1/4), warning against copying a kernel shape by name.

4. MoE intermediate layout / FP32 storage: see detailed accounting below. Candidate work is storage/lifetime reduction only after numerics are tested; the XMX GEMM's FP32 accumulation does not require writing every output row to a four-byte global buffer. However, the existing lower-precision `Hh` already covers the between-GU and down projection stage in the non-MMQ XMX path. Do not conflate that with the MMQ path's F32 `H` before quantization.

5. CPU AVX2: `src/kernels/cpu/native_expert.cpp`, `src/kernels/cpu/iq_avx2_rows.inl`, and CPU expert dispatch. The native i-quant/Q2_0 paths are the most architecture-portable candidates for a 5600X because AVX2 is shared by the test host and target CPU. Confirm dispatch is based on CPU feature bits and compare the same formats, thread count, and expert-row shapes; Xe's P/E-core affinity and AVX-512/SDE results do not transfer to a six-core Zen 3 host. Xe's README reports IQ2_S per-expert CPU time at one P-core for 1/2/4 tokens as 0.242/0.360/0.488 ms before and 0.242/0.253/0.382 ms after; IQ2_XXS 0.203/0.254/0.355 to 0.203/0.252/0.344 ms. These are B70 machine CPU microbenchmarks, not Ryzen predictions. AVX2 implementation may be worth differential parity testing; speed remains unverified here.

FP32 storage and lifetime (actual D versus Xe)

D's `prefill.cpp` defines N=2560, K=10 (line 114). `moe_bufs` at lines 1017–1027 returns MMQ capacities of T*K*1280 floats for GU and T*K*640 floats for H; the count-only layout at 1033–1044 adds T*K*N float Dm and conditionally Xq/Hq byte quantized rows. At allocation lines 1340–1350, `m.GU` and `m.Dm` are always float; `m.H` is float when MMQ is present; Xq/Hq are byte; Xs/Hh use uint16 on fallback/non-MMQ. In the MMQ group path lines 4939–4952 / 2078–2095, quantized weights and q8 input produce F32 GU, F32 SwiGLU H, quantized q8 Hq, then F32 Dm. Thus F32 H is transient until q8 quantization, but GU and Dm are full routed-row buffers. In the non-MMQ path, inputs/dequantized weights and Hh are half (`uint16_t`); GU and Dm remain float outputs. The Xe allocator similarly reserves F32 GU/Dm at lines 760–762 and F32 H at 765–768 when MMQ is active; it also retains half Xs/Hh where fallback applies. The non-MMQ Xe XMX group path at lines 2105–2129 dequantizes expert weights to half, calls grouped XMX GEMM into F32 GU, converts SwiGLU output into half Hh, then the down projection writes F32 Dm. This gives a specific lower-precision intermediate, Hh, while retaining FP32 GEMM accumulation/output for the other two matrices.

For K=10, one routed token's nominal capacity is GU 51,200 B + Dm 102,400 B + MMQ H 25,600 B = 179,200 B (175 KiB), before quantized rows, attention/GDN allocations, resident weights, and allocator padding. At T=12,288, these three arrays total 2,202,009,600 B = 2.05 GiB; GU alone is 600 MiB, Dm 1.17 GiB, H 300 MiB. At T=32,768 the same full-capacity estimate is 5.47 GiB (GU 1.56 GiB + Dm 3.05 GiB + H 0.76 GiB). These are allocation capacities, not guaranteed touched rows or incremental live bytes: buffers occupy a reused region sized as the maximum of GDN/QSA/MoE sets, not the sum of those sets; MoE's arrays coexist during MoE, while attention scratch occupies the same arena at its phase. Other separately allocated weights, expert staging/ring, KV and runtime allocations still consume VRAM. Therefore a full 32K MoE region of this nominal shape alone would leave little room on 10 GB, but whether it is allocated at that capacity depends on selected chunk T, CPU/GPU split, plan and fit checks. Inspect allocation plan on target; do not infer fit from B70.

A possible reduction is store F32 XMX result to half/bfloat16 immediately after a verified F32 accumulation, use that lower-precision result for SwiGLU/down, and retain F32 Dm only if downstream requires it. This can cut GU from 4 to 2 bytes/element (at T=12,288, save up to 300 MiB; at 32,768, 0.78 GiB). H is already transient then requantized in MMQ; replacing H with half before q8 quantization might save its corresponding 150/384 MiB but changes quantization inputs and needs output/parity tests. Do not reduce Dm without confirming the consumer and error budget; it feeds the mixed/residual accumulation. D's existing `Hh` is the already implemented lower-precision precedent. Full-precision accumulation inside the XMX operation and lower-precision global storage are distinct choices; inspect numerical semantics and compare generated tokens, logits/error thresholds, and fault status before claiming equivalence. No such change was benchmarked in R288.

Current B570 feasibility and reliability gates
The 10-GB card must also hold active model/resident expert weights, KV, runtime, staging/ring and buffers; the 5600X has fewer CPU cores than the i7-14700 and changes CPU-sharing balance. Xe source includes runtime memory/largest-allocation checks and tunable limits, but those do not qualify our D allocator, queue ordering, USM use, or graph/copy reuse. For any port: independently validate device and host USM allocations, largest allocation and reserve at actual chunk, event lifetime before arena reuse, and fallback when XMX/joint_matrix capability is absent. Current evidence is one fresh 32K route-census diagnostic and not a performance or 256K qualification. No end-to-end speed conclusion is supported.
