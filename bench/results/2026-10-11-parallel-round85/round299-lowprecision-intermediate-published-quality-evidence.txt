# R299 — Public quality evidence for low-precision intermediate storage

Date: 2026-10-11
Scope: public quality evidence for FP16/BF16/FP8 storage of recurrent state, residual hidden state, and attention/MLP intermediates relevant to Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Qwen3.5, and Qwen3-Next. The focus is persistent storage rounding: compute an update, round/store it, then read the rounded value in a later operation. This differs from rounding GEMM operands while retaining wider accumulation, weight quantization, or KV-cache quantization.

## Finding

There is no public quantitative ability result found for simple FP16/BF16/FP8 storage of recurrent state on the exact Qwen3.8-Flash-Next or Qwen3-Next model, nor for attention/MLP intermediate stores on those models. For a closely related model, Qwen3.5, direct recent recurrent-state experiments show that naive per-token BF16 storage can cause large task-score losses. A separate Qwen3.6 study finds FP16 state storage close to FP32 on its evaluation, while BF16 is mixed and FP8 E4M3 can be severely worse. A Qwen3.8-27B study likewise finds large losses from uniform low-bit state storage, but near-baseline results from a specialized calibrated state codec. These results do not justify assuming that a simple low-precision cast has only tiny ability cost; they also do not establish that FP16 is unsafe on the exact target models.

The strongest near-match for residual hidden-state storage is still the Qwen3.8 technical report's qualitative claim that FP8 storage of gated-residual (GR) states has almost no loss. It does not provide benchmark scores, a complete precision/storage protocol, a context-length distribution, or an exact Flash-Next comparison in the reported statement. No quantitative same-model attention/MLP intermediate-storage result was located. R289 already records that claim and its limits; this report adds the recurrent-state papers below rather than treating them as a substitute for GR or transient activation evidence.

These papers repeatedly round and reuse the recurrent state, so their large BF16/FP8 losses cannot be dismissed as GEMM accumulation differences. At the same time, their varying results show format, model, update rule, and task all matter. LeapQuant and STEPQuant's successful methods include blockwise calibration, buffering, mixed precision, or compensators; their scores are not an allowance for an uncalibrated cast.

## Residual and ordinary activation storage

The [Qwen3.8 technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04098) qualitatively reports near-zero loss for FP8 storage of GR residual states, but the cited claim supplies no metric table or adequate measurement detail to turn “near-zero” into a measurable loss bound. Its GR residual state is not automatically equivalent to a GDN/KDA recurrent state, a temporary attention/MLP tensor stored between kernels, or an output rounded after a wide-accumulator GEMM. There is no directly comparable public quality table for FP16/BF16/FP8 attention or MLP intermediate stores on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Qwen3.5, or Qwen3-Next in the sources located for this round.

Evidence about W8A8/NVFP4 GEMM operands, quantized weights, or KV cache is excluded from the state-storage conclusions: those formats change a different tensor or compute stage. Likewise, a stated FP32 accumulation mode does not itself establish that the stored output or subsequent recurrent state remains FP32.

## Decision relevance

For an ability-loss allowance described only as “tiny,” public evidence does not support shipping a naive BF16 or FP8 recurrent-state store without a target-model quality comparison. FP16 is a plausible candidate based on DAMP's adjacent-model results, but the Qwen3.5 BF16 results and model-specific spread make it unsafe to infer a precise target-model loss from analogy. The Qwen3.8 GR-residual statement is encouraging but too underspecified to qualify the same change. No current evidence qualifies arbitrary hidden-state/attention/MLP scratch stores.

A target-model comparison should separately test (1) recurrence state written after every update and read back on the next token, (2) cross-layer residual storage, and (3) attention/MLP intermediate output storage. Keep update/accumulation arithmetic fixed when attributing storage-rounding effects. Include the intended context/generation lengths and task classes, and compare per-task changes against the user's explicit ability-loss allowance. Treat numerical check failures and task-quality loss as distinct outcomes.

## Source status

The three recurrent-state papers above are public arXiv manuscripts; the report makes no claim that they have completed peer review. The cited Qwen3.8 technical report is a primary model report. Values are transcribed from the cited papers' tables/figures; this round ran no model, benchmark, build, or GPU test. This file is a literature review only.
