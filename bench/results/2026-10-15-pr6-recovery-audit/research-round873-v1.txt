# R873 — Stationary dataflows and array utilization in non-LLM inference ASICs

**Classification:** Knowledge collection; keep this report body held until its assigned reviewer records scope, depth, novelty, source checks, and retrieval metadata.

## Question and scope

For a dense fully connected layer, write the operation as `Y[B,M] = X[B,K] × W[K,M]`. The question is how weight-stationary (WS), output-stationary (OS), row-stationary (RS), and flexible spatial mappings trade reuse against partial-sum movement and idle processing elements as batch `B`, reduction depth `K`, output width `M`, array dimensions, and local storage change. These are architecture-level mechanisms for non-LLM inference. The quantitative examples below are from an analytical Eyeriss comparison and counters on deployed TPU v1; neither is a desktop GPU result.

A useful first-order traffic bound is one read of each input and weight tensor and one write of outputs: `bX·B·K + bW·K·M + bY·B·M` bytes at a chosen memory boundary, for `B·K·M` MACs. The bound requires retaining or forwarding each reused value inside that boundary. If `K` is split into `ceil(K/Kt)` reduction tiles and the `B×M` partial sums cannot remain local, each intermediate tile can add a partial-sum read and write, approaching `2·bP·B·M·(ceil(K/Kt)−1)` bytes at that boundary, in addition to final output traffic. A tile's basic storage demand is approximately `bW·Kt·Mt + bX·Bt·Kt + bP·Bt·Mt`; double buffering and routing FIFOs raise it. This is a derived accounting model, not measured data. It explains why more buffer may rescue reuse while shrinking the PE count under a fixed area budget, and why more PEs do not automatically improve latency when the mapped dimensions or bandwidth cannot feed them.

## Mechanisms

| Mapping | What stays local | Reuse and reduction pattern | Where it tends to fit / fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| WS | Weight (or weight tile) in PE/register | Reuses each weight over batch rows and streams activation values; output partial sums must be accumulated or forwarded elsewhere. | Strong when `B` or repeated work amortizes weight fetch and the partial-sum working set fits. A small accumulator/global buffer can cap concurrent outputs or cause spills. |
| OS | Output accumulator in PE/register | Keeps a `Y[b,m]` partial sum local while looping over `K`; streams weights and activations. Operand multicast can reuse an activation across `M` and a weight across `B`. | Strong when enough independent outputs occupy the array and accumulators fit. Narrow `B` or `M` can leave lanes idle; each operand still needs delivery bandwidth. |
| RS | A short row/primitive of operands and partial sums, with reuse across neighboring PEs | Balances weight, activation, and partial-sum reuse using local storage plus spatial forwarding. In FC layers there is no sliding-window convolution reuse, so the mapping must spend its array and buffer on batch/output/reduction parallelism instead. | Can outperform a single-purpose mapping when local storage and forwarding capture several reuse types. Its advantage is shape- and resource-dependent, not inherent to the name. |
| Flexible spatial mapping | No single tensor is fixed as stationary; a mapper chooses loop order, tiles, spatial axes, and reduction placement per layer | Can choose WS-like, OS-like, or mixed mappings and route multicasts/reductions to fit the current `B,K,M`. | Helps with changing shapes and tails if the hardware routes data that way. Switches, routing bandwidth, reduction paths, mapping constraints, and search cost are real costs. |

The labels do not uniquely specify a schedule: OS can distribute input reuse spatially, while WS can use global storage for partial sums. MAESTRO and Timeloop model loop order, spatialization, tiling, buffers, and communication; Timeloop cannot discover mappings excluded by its constraints.

Equal-area does not mean equal buffer capacity: Eyeriss fixes PE count and storage area but reallocates area between RF and global buffer, whose area-per-byte differs. For an array mapped over output and batch tiles, an idealized lane bound is `min(Mt,PM)/PM × min(Bt,PB)/PB`; reduction-axis mappings have different bounds. This assumes adequate `K` and delivery bandwidth, with no fill bubbles. A flexible mapping can change the factorization to fill lanes, at the cost of potentially longer partial-sum routes.

## Evidence and counterexamples

**Stationary reuse can win when dimensions and batch expose it.** Chen, Emer, and Sze compare WS, OS variants, RS, and no local reuse on AlexNet at equal PE count and storage *area*, optimizing each mapping analytically. FC shapes are `B×9216 · 9216×4096`, `B×4096 · 4096×4096`, and `B×4096 · 4096×1000` (FC1 input is 256×6×6 flattened). Figure 14 uses 16-bit words, 1,024 PEs, and aggregates the three FC layers at `B=16,64,256`. Reference allocation is 512 B RF/PE plus 512 KiB global buffer; mappings reallocate equal storage area, so total bytes vary. The model reports RS at least 1.3× lower energy at batch 16 and up to 2.8× at batch 256. It counts MAC and DRAM, global-buffer, inter-PE, and RF access costs using 65-nm estimates. Its EDP uses delay reciprocal to active PE count, not measured FC latency. The separate fabricated Eyeriss chip has 168 PEs, 0.5 KiB RF/PE, 108 KiB buffer, 16-bit fixed-point, 200 MHz, and 65-nm CMOS; it validates RS functionality, not the 1,024-PE cross-dataflow result. [Eyeriss, ISCA 2016](https://d1qx31qr3h6wln.cloudfront.net/publications/ISCA_2016_Eyeriss.pdf)

**Counterexample 1: at small batch, reuse cannot repay weight traffic.** In that Eyeriss experiment, at `B=1` all dataflows have approximately equal energy because DRAM weight reads dominate; the batch sweep starts at 16 because one-example reuse is low. From the published AlexNet dimensions and 16-bit words, weights total about 112 MiB, versus the 512 KiB reference global buffer. Thus the larger-batch energy ratios do not transfer to one-request inference without resident weights.

**Counterexample 2: array specialization can expose the wrong axis.** Eyeriss reports that its OSA mapping performs poorly on FC because it expects input pixels from one spatial plane, while FC layers have very small spatial extent. In the AlexNet comparison above, the other OS variant and RS can still arrange reuse differently, but the result shows that “keep outputs stationary” does not guarantee useful PE occupancy if the mapping's spatial axis is poorly matched to flattened FC work. More generally, if a fixed array assigns `PM` lanes to output channels for an `M` smaller than `PM`, those lanes are idle for that tile; distributing over `B` or `K` may recover work but can create reductions or different operand traffic. This last statement is a mapping consequence, not an Eyeriss measurement.

**Counterexample 3: a deployed systolic array can remain mostly idle on MLP inference.** TPU v1 has a fixed 256×256 array of 65,536 8-bit MACs, 4 MiB of 32-bit accumulators, a 24 MiB unified buffer, and two 64 KiB weight tiles to hide a 256-cycle shift. Its instruction multiplies `B×256` by `256×256` to produce `B×256`. Production MLP0 has five FC layers and MLP1 four; they have 20M/5M weights and batches 200/168. Individual matrix shapes are not published. Counters over the app runs report 12.7%/10.6% array-active cycles, 12.5%/9.4% useful MACs as a fraction of peak, and 53.9%/44.2% weight-stall cycles. These are measured device counters, not simulated layer estimates. Roofline analysis calls both MLPs memory-bound, but the paper does not isolate one cause for every inactive cycle. [TPU v1, ISCA 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.04760v1)

Increasing `B` raises weight reuse toward `B·M` uses per loaded weight, but also multiplies activation and partial-sum working sets. If accumulators or global storage cannot hold `B×Mt`, partial sums spill or fewer output tiles run concurrently. Eyeriss' WS model notes that buffered partial sums limit on-chip filters and reports a convolution batch-64 case that could not run due to buffer size; that is not a direct FC result. For FC, the direct evidence here is the single-example weight-traffic floor and TPU MLP stalls.

## Flexible mapping and limits

MAESTRO uses spatial/temporal directives to estimate reuse, occupancy, buffers, NoC bandwidth, time, and energy. Timeloop searches a constrained mapspace over compute, memory, and links. Both can compare FC mappings, but assumptions about hardware, costs, bandwidth, and allowed mappings limit these model outputs. MAERI illustrates hardware flexibility through configurable distribution and reduction trees that route different multicasts and partial sums; switches, wires, control, and contention cost area and power. Its utilization headline spans heterogeneous cases, not an equal-area FC comparison, so it is not used as a quantitative MLP claim. [MAESTRO, MICRO 2019](https://d1qx31qr3h6wln.cloudfront.net/publications/MICRO_2019_Maestro.pdf); [Timeloop, ISPASS 2019](https://accelergy.mit.edu/timeloop.pdf); [MAERI, ASPLOS 2018](https://anands09.github.io/papers/maeri_asplos2018.pdf)

Fixed WS suits repeated dense tiles when `K` and `M` fill the array, reuse keeps weights local, and partial sums fit or reduce cheaply. OS suits enough independent outputs with local K-wise accumulation. RS or mixed mapping helps when several operands and partial sums can use low-cost local paths. Flexibility matters when successive shapes change the short axis, but only if buffers and routes can support the alternate mapping. Tiny batches, narrow outputs, tails, accumulator pressure, and weight-bandwidth limits each defeat a different stationary assumption. Eyeriss is a 16-bit AlexNet energy model; TPU v1 is an 8-bit deployed workload from 2017. These are mechanism comparisons, not product or desktop-GPU claims.
