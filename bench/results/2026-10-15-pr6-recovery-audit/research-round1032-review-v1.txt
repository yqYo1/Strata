# Round 1032 — QSA head-parallel resource and profiler retrieval review

## Question and current evidence

Target supplied for this review: Intel Arc B570 10 GiB, Ryzen 5 5600X, Level Zero driver `1.17.39395+14`, Intel LLVM 22.1 build from compiler binary SHA-256 `1290881b4e12a5e002e5b0de0787f85328ee5fe19f253a54be1cdd97dc2f26eb`; VTune is not on `PATH`. The compute queue already enables SYCL event profiling. Main asks whether the QSA score kernel's roughly 25% component-time improvement from head-parallel mapping comes from useful arithmetic/shuffle work, cache or memory service, or register/resource effects.

The compact head-parallel cohort is complete: three fresh processes per arm and context, ten timed score/top-k pairs per process, for synthetic seeded inputs at contexts 32,768, 78,453 and 262,144. Score stage medians change 2.585 to 1.954 ms (-24.4%), 6.101 to 4.568 ms (-25.1%), and 20.231 to 15.124 ms (-25.2%). Top-k remains about 0.46, 1.20 and 5.08 ms; paired score-plus-top-k latency improves about 20–21%. Scores and selected IDs match bit-for-bit and there are no fresh faults. The cohort explicitly labels these monotonic submit-plus-wait stage totals, excludes first dispatch, and leaves physical traffic, register pressure, spills and occupancy unmeasured. It has `production_selected=false` and `model_PP_measured=false`; these are promising component timings, not a cause attribution or a model result.

The separate first-dispatch diagnostic used one traced fresh process per arm at context 32,768 / 256 queries. It saw roughly 87–100 ms submit wall and 86–99 ms submit-thread CPU for the first score stage, about 3.8–4.9 ms waiting, and 302–312 ms submit wall for first top-k. Cache insertion was visible. UR tracing, parameter validation and `SYCL_CACHE_TRACE=2` were enabled; this is not a steady-state run, does not explain the integrated engine's first request, and says nothing about the steady-state kernel's resource limit.

The head-parallel build receipt pins the exact compiler and binary and strict floating-point flags (`-O3`, `-fno-fast-math`, `-ffp-contract=off`, `-fsycl`, warnings as errors). It does not include device ISA/IR, per-kernel GRF/register usage, spill data, static/dynamic SLM or occupancy/resource output. The source review says the candidate increases logical key loads and live values, but those are source facts, not physical bytes or register counts. No device-resource report for this exact candidate is indexed.
