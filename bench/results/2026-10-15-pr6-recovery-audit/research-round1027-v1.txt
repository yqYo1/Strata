## Designs and tradeoffs

**Hierarchical multi-pass scan.** Each work-group scans a tile, writes one aggregate, and exits. A later kernel scans the O(N/B) aggregate array; a final pass applies each tile's carry to local prefixes (possibly fused into the consumer). This tolerates grids larger than concurrent residency because no group spins on another group in the same launch. Kernel/graph dependencies form global phase boundaries. Scratch is small, but traffic includes an extra pass over input/output or output adjustment, and one or more launches. Tiny N may be launch-bound; large bandwidth-bound N may pay for the extra transfer. This is the portable baseline when only within-group progress is relied upon.

**Single-pass decoupled lookback.** Merrill and Garland's CUB algorithm computes tile aggregates, publishes tile status, and looks backward for a prefix or enough aggregate states to derive the carry. It permits redundant addition to decouple local work from prefix propagation. The paper reports about 2N input/output movement (read N, write N), versus roughly 3N for conventional reduce-then-scan, and bandwidth-near results on evaluated NVIDIA GPUs. This refutes “an extra pass is always faster,” but only for its algorithm/protocol on tested NVIDIA hardware. A naive predecessor chain deadlocks if resident high-ID groups spin on an unscheduled low-ID producer and occupy its slots. Oversubscription permits this absent an ordering/residency contract. Redundant lookback bounds dependency depth; it does not make arbitrary spinning safe.

**Bounded persistent/cooperative work.** A persistent kernel processes many tiles through a bounded resident worker set; an explicitly cooperative grid can synchronize only when the launch API guarantees co-residency and supports the barrier. It amortizes launches and may keep queue metadata on-device, but adds queue contention, long-lived resource use, and imbalance. Too few workers underfill; more than concurrent residency can deadlock at a global barrier. CUDA 13.2 has an explicit NVIDIA cooperative launch: `cudaLaunchCooperativeKernel` enables grid sync and constrains simultaneous residency; oversized launches may fail (§4.4.8). Ordinary CUDA launches have no such all-grid barrier. This guarantee is not transferable to SYCL/Xe2 by analogy.

No universal ranking: tiny inputs may favor one launch; large inputs can favor justified lookback; persistent workers fit when queue and consumer stay resident. Measure the full dependency path.

## Progress and memory contract

SYCL 2020 revision 12 (2026-08-06, §§3.8.3.4, 3.9.4, 3.9.8) gives each work-item at least weakly parallel forward progress; stronger progress is implementation-defined. It requires that reaching a group barrier not prevent other members of that same group from progressing. That is not a promise that an earlier-numbered work-group runs while another group spins. A work-group barrier synchronizes only the group; portable code also cannot assume subgroup scheduling/forward-progress details. Device-scope atomics affect visibility and ordering, not scheduling fairness.

For device-wide status, write payload then publish state with device-scope release; poll with compatible acquire and read only after the correct state. Relaxed publication, work-group scope between groups, or a plain flag is insufficient. Distinguish `aggregate-ready` from `prefix-ready`; memory order cannot fix a logical state error. Initialize before launch, avoid reuse while readers remain, and prevent ABA with per-invocation storage or nonrepeating generation. Check atomic_ref alignment, address-space and supported scope on the backend.

CUDA 13.2's scoped atomics specify C++-style release/acquire communication across blocks at device scope: an acquire that reads the release publication may consume preceding data. This is a visibility contract, not a scheduling guarantee. Cooperative launch is a separate constrained facility. CUB's result demonstrates a carefully engineered NVIDIA implementation; neither its paper nor CUDA ordering alone proves a home-grown algorithm portable.

Xe2/Level Zero does not add a public SYCL grid barrier. Intel oneAPI GPU Optimization Guide 2025.2 (2025-07-10) covers execution, occupancy, SLM, and subgroups, not general cross-group fairness. A DPC++ extension must be named with its version/device contract. No reviewed Intel documentation established portable Xe2 global progress or CUDA-equivalent cooperative sync. Subgroup width, residency, atomic lowering and costs must be checked on the actual stack.

## Deadlines and lifetime

A host deadline is not device cancellation. Freeing a flag, payload, or output after timeout while the kernel may still access it is use-after-free. The kernel may be starved, preempted, slow, or uncancellable. Retain allocations until an event/fence proves completion; quarantine timed-out generations until retirement; or avoid intra-kernel waits and use command dependencies (still retaining memory until all commands finish). A device timeout flag can make waiters exit, but does not force a producer to run. On timeout, partial output must be marked invalid; prove every group exits and the kernel completes before reclaiming storage. Context reset is a distinct recovery event; free only after reset completion.

## When extra passes win

Traffic estimates omit status atomics, polling, dependent loads, redundant reductions, stalls, and occupancy spent spinning. Regular phases may achieve higher useful bandwidth. Conversely, launches dominate tiny/cache-resident cases; tile-local consumer fusion may remove materialization. Lookback is strongest when N is large, status fits cache, redundant work is bounded, and progress is justified. Persistent work can win if it removes host/launch overhead without losing occupancy.

R759 gives B570 product specs of 18 Xe-cores and 10 GB GDDR6, not scan bandwidth or resident-group count. Do not infer cooperative capacity from Xe-core count; query resources/limits on the exact stack. The pinned application snapshot is not scan evidence. No B570 query or scan benchmark was performed.

## Affine recurrence connection

For scalar x[t+1] = a[t]x[t]+b[t], represent a step by (a,b). Composition, (a2,b2) after (a1,b1) = (a2a1, a2b1+b2), is associative over exact real arithmetic, so a prefix scan computes transforms for each state. This is standard scan mathematics, not an implementation claim.

A Qwen-like gated-delta update is schematically S' = λS + βk(v−kᵀS) = (λI−βkkᵀ)S + βkvᵀ. Affine maps over flattened S compose associatively in real arithmetic, but general composition is state-dimension-squared and can cost far more than the low-rank sequential update. Floating reassociation changes rounding, FMA contraction, underflow, and error; storage precision and chunk handoff matter too. Algebraic equality is not numerical equality. Sequential per-head work remains reasonable for short chunks or when parallel head work is sufficient and scan costs exceed saved serial depth. R592/R693 historical tests do not validate a Xe2 scan.

## Falsification gates for a future study

Record operator/identity, segment convention, N, width, tile, consumer, lifetime, and versions. Test empty/single input, tails, adversarial values, reuse, bounds, and concurrency. Inject delayed/missing predecessors, oversubscription, stalled producers, generation reuse, and timeout; retain memory through proven completion. Check release/acquire transitions. For cooperative work, prove worst-case compiled residency and test rejection/fallback. Time through the first consumer; record samples, bytes, polling/atomics, bandwidth, and resources. Separate portable SYCL claims from pinned Xe2 DPC++/Level Zero results. A two-pass win falsifies bandwidth-only reasoning; a stalled-producer hang rejects lookback regardless of average speed.
