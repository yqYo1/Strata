# R813 — Knowledge collection: bounded native16 grouped MoE

**Purpose:** knowledge collection. **Evidence status:** source facts, arithmetic, and architecture hypotheses only. No builds, tests, GPU queries, profiler work, process inspection, or performance measurements were run. This report does not read or rely on R810's live implementation package and does not use XeStrata material.

## Question and scope

The question is how to schedule a routed layer when packed IQ3 weights stay authoritative, native 16-bit weights are materialized only for a bounded expert group, and the group may serve several token chunks before eviction. This extends R807's dense GEMM seam into grouped execution and narrows the prior dequant-cache, expert-wave, small-M census, and grouped-GEMM surveys (R601, R621–R623, R627–R628, R633, R701–R702). The new join is residency policy with actual expert-row counts, reuse lifetime, and a fused gate/up boundary.

I use the supplied Strata per-expert shapes: gate/up `1280×2560`, down `2560×640`; the gate/up output has two 640-wide halves. One FP16 expert therefore occupies `(1280×2560 + 2560×640)×2 = 9,830,400 bytes = 9.375 MiB`. Materializing all 512 experts for **one layer** is `512×9,830,400 = 5,033,164,800 bytes = 4.6875 GiB`. A 32-expert slot group costs 300 MiB; 64 costs 600 MiB. These are payload arithmetic, not allocator admission or proof that 512 experts are routed in a prompt. The layer's packed bank, any resident packed experts from other layers, KV, 16-bit state/residuals, route metadata, partial outputs, scratch, workspaces, and safety margin also consume the 10 GiB budget.

Let `R_j` be the actual routed `(token, expert)` rows in chunk `j`; let `m[e,j]` be the rows routed to expert `e`, and `E_j={e:m[e,j]>0}`. No fixed top-k or all-expert assumption is needed. If `B_other` is all concurrently live memory except the FP16 expert staging set, then a first capacity bound is `G_max=floor((10 GiB-B_other)/9.375 MiB)`, clamped to experts actually selected for the current superchunk. `B_other` must be measured from the real request and allocator; this formula cannot establish that a proposed `G` fits.

## What the source shows

At vLLM-XPU revision `6b30c9b92cc525c32729df988b5f918e7c5661a5`, the Xe2 grouped helper schedules tiles across experts with per-expert row counts, prefix sums of row-tile counts, and a device atomic work counter. The launcher chooses its 16-bit policy using `A_total_M / num_experts`: averages at most 8 choose the named M16 policy, at most 16 choose M32, and larger averages choose N-oriented or default tiles according to N. The scheduler can keep workers busy across ragged expert M sizes, but the policy remains one shape per launch; its average can hide a hot expert among many empty or cold experts. The wrapper also binds Torch allocations/current-stream handling and assumes device index zero for its workgroup sizing, so its full host wrapper is not a Strata queue contract. Its grouped device and interface files have BSD-3-Clause headers. The policy header has no SPDX marker; treat its provenance as unresolved and do not reuse it without a separate file-origin review.

SGLang at `4cca7fcd3e644f22a61c7a6e6b57bb89b568d62a` provides adjacent evidence. Its config lookup is keyed by expert count, shape, quantization, device and M, and chooses the nearest configured M; fallback includes M16/N32/K64 under small-M conditions. The dispatcher aligns actual route IDs into expert/block metadata. Its `M` is hidden-state token count and a config key, not the observed `m[e,j]` histogram. Separately, the fused-SwiGLU kernel accumulates gate/up in FP32, casts the accumulator to compute type, then applies SiLU×up and stores only half-width results, removing the two-half intermediate and a standalone activation launch. That cast is a precision boundary to check, not blindly copy. The fused path's caller has eligibility checks, including BF16 and an unquantized route; it still writes a post-activation tensor for down GEMM. This is a producer epilogue reference, not an IQ3 native16 path or down-projection fusion. The repository is Apache-2.0; the kernel file has an Apache-2.0 marker.

Intel LLM Scaler's pinned SGLang FP8 prefill source (`49c94a32bed69fbabd824debc4b136a6d15cae6d`) makes the M-tail problem concrete: it bins each expert's sorted route rows into fixed `MAX_M` chunks and launches one work item per `(chunk, N-tile)`, rather than serially looping over a hot expert's M blocks. Its chunk capacity is bounded by `floor(total_rows/MAX_M)+num_experts+1`, using the sum of `ceil(m[e]/MAX_M)` as the actual work count. The source converts FP8 weights to half in the consuming tile, accumulates in FP32, and computes gate/up activation before storing the intermediate. It can share one chunk table between up and down when their configured chunk sizes match; up and down remain separate kernels, so this is metadata reuse rather than whole-MLP fusion. The file has no per-file SPDX line; the repository root is Apache-2.0, and its comment attributes it to another in-repository int4 reference. This is read-only design evidence, not a cleared source-reuse proposal.

## Three bounded candidates

1. **Count-aware grouped tile dispatch.** Build a GPU route histogram and stable row lists once per layer superchunk; skip `m[e]=0`, then bucket nonempty experts by observed row count and dispatch M8/M16/M32/large kernels or scheduler policies per bucket. A useful padding measure is `η_M = Σ_e ceil(m[e]/BM)×BM / Σ_e m[e]`; task count is approximately `Σ_e ceil(m[e]/BM)×ceil(N/BN)` for each projection. Smaller `BM` can raise `η_M` and expose more parallel work, while also increasing tile/task count and schedule metadata. Compare histogram-driven buckets against a single average-M policy; don't import vLLM's thresholds as Strata thresholds. A giant expert may need subchunks even after bucketing, as in the Intel source.

2. **Bounded group residency across a chunk superbatch.** Gather route metadata for `Q` consecutive chunks, group only the experts present in their union, and stage/dequantize at most `G≤G_max` experts at a time. For each resident group, run its gate/up projection, SwiGLU, and down projection for all matching rows in that superbatch, then release the staged pair before loading the next group. This creates a plausible reuse window without a full-layer half-weight bank. If expert `e` is requested in `p_e` chunks but is materialized `r_e` times before eviction, saved decode work is proportional to `Σ_e (p_e-r_e)×D_e`, where `D_e` is that expert's quantized-to-FP16 decode cost; it must exceed staging, buffer traffic, metadata, launch, and synchronization overhead. A small LRU can still thrash when chunk order changes; R601 already flags that route walks must actually revisit residents. Group-major order can also change FP32 contribution order. Keep FP32 output accumulation and preserve the source route/add order or qualify its numerical effect.

3. **Native16 gate/up fused epilogue.** Keep the IQ3 decode and dot product in the producer, maintain FP32 gate/up accumulators and activation math, and store only the 640-wide post-SwiGLU result in BF16 or FP16. For each routed row that replaces a two-half output of `2×640×2=2,560 bytes` with `640×2=1,280 bytes`, saving 1,280 bytes of intermediate storage/write traffic plus the separate activation launch, before allocator padding. The down projection still needs the complete 640 values and remains a consumer kernel; fusing it through the gate/up tile would require retaining/recomputing all activated channels across the down reduction and is a separate, higher-risk design. Verify the actual Strata gate/up row order, activation semantics, router scaling point, and rounding boundary before treating SGLang's interleaved BF16 path as equivalent.

## Memory and work trade-off

The packed IQ3 source remains compact, while the half staging bank is 2 bytes per value. A policy that expands the entire 512-expert layer adds 4.6875 GiB on top of all other live storage; it is not a viable default inference from a nominal 10 GiB device. The admission inequality is `B_packed + B_stage(G) + B_KV + B_state/residual + B_route + B_partial + B_other_scratch + B_workspaces + B_slack ≤ 10 GiB`, where `B_stage(G)=G×9.375 MiB`. Unknown terms stay unknown until the live allocation map is recorded. Releasing a group frees staging payload only after its last consumer completes; it does not prove physical free memory returns immediately.

Chunk reuse is a decode-versus-bandwidth trade. Inline decode repeats packed reads and IQ arithmetic for each M tile. Full staging decodes once per expert residency, but half weights are larger than IQ3 and still must be read by later GEMM tiles. Compare `Σ tile (packed-load + decode + dot)` with `group-load/decode/store + Σ tile (half-load + dot) + route-pack + sync`; timing just the dot omits the decision. If the same expert is used in many adjacent chunks while its group stays resident, decode reuse may dominate; if groups churn or each expert has few rows, the larger half-weight traffic and staging can lose. For a `Q`-chunk window, route-list storage and intermediate/partial-output buffers scale with actual routed rows `Σ_j |R_j|`, not with 512, and belong in the budget.

## Ownership and qualification boundary

The caller should own the queue, the quantized source span, route counts/IDs, row-list buffers, staged weights, activated rows, FP32 per-token contribution state, and terminal events. On one in-order queue, submit route histogram/scan, source transfer if needed, group dequant/compute, and scatter in dependency order; retain each allocation and host transfer source through the final event that reads it. Before reusing a slot, wait on or depend on its previous consumer event. A separate queue needs explicit producer-event waits and a completion handoff; never host-reset a device scheduler counter or overwrite route metadata while an earlier kernel may still read them. Initialize counters in the same ordered queue or use generation-tagged disjoint counters. This is a lifetime contract, not a reason to construct a reset/hang experiment; the earlier separate-queue native16 failure remains unresolved.

All candidates need an FP32-reference comparison of full output rows, router-weight application, tails, zero-row experts, route-order effects, allocation faults, and repeated-chunk lifetime. Native16 format selection needs range/task evidence for both FP16 and BF16. The reported R803 custom native16 GEMMs were 5.57–30.66× slower than the old oneMKL F32-output route on paired shapes; that makes the path worth measuring, but says nothing about whether grouping, staging, or fusion wins. Actual runtime, quality, and performance remain **UNRUN** for this report.
