# Geometric GPU neighbor search: cells, stencils and hierarchies

## Scope and evidence boundary

This collection compares fixed-radius geometric neighbor search for moving particles. It distinguishes finding candidates, building a reusable Verlet list, and the force steps that consume that list: a fast query can lose after construction or extra candidates, while a faster list build may only modestly improve a full simulation. Published comparisons are from NVIDIA CUDA GPUs; they do not establish performance on another GPU or engine. No local implementation or performance result was examined.

## Methods and phase crossovers

| Method | Attractive workload and principal costs | Where it loses |
|---|---|---|
| Direct all-pairs | No index or list build, regular O(N²) comparisons; can suit small N, high density, or a cutoff near box size, especially if force is fused. This crossover is algorithmic inference, not measured in cited GPU studies. | Pair work repeats every step; wastes effort when few pairs are within cutoff. |
| Uniform cells | Low build cost and simple, regular indexing for one cutoff and fairly even occupancy. | Cell stencil admits many non-neighbors; sparse boxes or tiny cutoffs add cell access and grid-storage overhead. |
| Stenciled cells | Fine bins plus per-type stencils prune cells outside each species' cutoff; useful when a small minority has longer range. | Many cells per stencil at high disparity; finer bins cut pair checks but multiply cell visits. |
| Linear / quantized BVH | Morton sort, build bounds, then traverse occupied regions; suits sparse, clustered or strongly size-disparate systems. Quantization reduces node traffic. | Sort/tree construction, divergence, bounding-box false positives and low per-query parallelism can outweigh pruning. |

Direct all-pairs is a useful small-system or dense/cutoff-near-box control, not a generally scalable neighbor finder. Its actual crossover depends on index setup, candidate rejection, force cost and list reuse over several steps. Neither GPU paper below times a fused direct-force alternative, so no numerical crossover follows from their asymptotic discussion.

## Measured evidence

Howard et al. compared GPU stenciled cells and LBVH for a 192,000-particle Lennard-Jones mixture on a Tesla K80, CUDA 7, single precision. The synthetic workload used a cubic periodic box, solvent cutoff r_AA=3, large-particle fraction x_B=10–50%, and r_BB=3–18. With zero list buffer, it rebuilt every step. For r_BB<6, stenciled cells matched the standard cell list while LBVH was slower: tree sorting and traversal cost more than the simpler bins. At larger disparity, both improved on a standard cell list, and LBVH beat stenciled cells particularly for r_BB>6 and x_B<0.3.

Their colloid/solvent case fixed solvent density at 0.6 and varied colloid diameter a=5, 10, 20 and volume fraction φ=0.1 or 0.2 in cubic periodic boxes. Solvent cutoff was 3; the largest colloid case reached a 50:3 interaction-range ratio. With list buffer 1.0, they profiled 25,000-step runs with roughly 1,200–1,400 rebuilds. LBVH list-build speedup over stenciled cells increased from 2.1× at a=5 to 8.0–8.2× at a=20, while whole-run speedup grew from 1.5× to 3.3–3.6×. At a=20, φ=0.1 the system had 100 colloids and 2,176,957 solvent particles. This is a hierarchy-favoring case, but its gains are specific to this workload and K80. [Howard et al. 2016](https://pccm.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf2196/files/media/irg-2a_04-panagiotopoulos.pdf)

Howard et al.'s later quantized-BVH study measured build plus query/traversal on cubic periodic systems, with 10 configurations, 200 warm-up iterations, then 500 measured iterations repeated five times. It used HOOMD-blue 2.4.1, CUDA, and K80, P100, V100 and GTX 1080 GPUs, for single- and double-precision particle data. For Lennard-Jones fluid (N=128,000, r_c=3, ρ=0.2 or 0.8), BVH build time was almost twice grid-build time, but faster traversal repaid it in most cases. A high-density, single-precision case was a slight grid win (0.9× BVH speedup in the paper's figure). The headline 2–4× BVH advantage describes the benchmark suite, not every device and precision. With double precision, quantized nodes were smaller than particle data and the advantage generally increased. For WCA with the much shorter r_c=2^(1/6)σ, low cell occupancy made grids less favorable. A 1,000,000-particle spinodal system with liquid/vapor density variation also generally favored BVH. Density, cutoff, precision and GPU throughput therefore matter alongside particle count. [Howard et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1901.08088v2)

The 2019 grid baseline used sorting to provide compact deterministic cell lists; the authors found it roughly twice as slow as HOOMD-blue's atomic-insertion grid in selected tests. Its BVH/grid ratios should not be transferred to every cell-list implementation. Conversely, the 2016 stenciled list already prunes type-inappropriate cells, and quantized BVH reduces node size from 32 to 16 bytes. Compare paired build, query, output and force phases rather than treating structure labels as portable speed rankings.

## Candidate work, memory and geometry

With cells of side r_c, a 3-D query checks a 3×3×3 neighborhood; the cutoff sphere occupies about 16% of that cube, so many candidate distances are rejected. A stencil can skip cells whose nearest point is outside the cutoff, but particles in intersecting cells still need checks. Howard et al. found that setting bin width to the shortest cutoff was best in almost all tested mixtures: halving it reduced short-range distance checks by about 40%, but caused nearly five times as many cell visits and made list building 50–100% slower. At low occupancy, cell visits return little useful work; tiny cutoffs can create many mostly empty cells.

A BVH pays for Morton-key generation, sorting, tree construction and bound fitting. It searches occupied objects rather than reserving every cell in empty space, which suits sparse or clustered distributions. Traversal is irregular and target-dependent, however, so GPU lanes can diverge. The 2016 implementation sorted particles spatially to make warp traversals more alike but used one thread per particle. The 2019 grid used cooperative threads per target; the BVH still used one. Small N can therefore underfill the GPU, even though the authors' V100 test beat their grid down to N=1,000.

Bounds trade exactness for cheap rejection. Conservative outward rounding avoids false negatives but admits false positives. The 2019 quantized design used 10-bit bounds, 16-byte nodes and padded single-precision query spheres. Its Lennard-Jones tests found about 1.5 extra neighbors per particle at ρ=0.2 and 3.8 at ρ=0.8 (roughly 5% more neighbors). Because traversal accepted leaf overlaps without an exact particle-distance test, extra candidates could also increase force work and list size. An exact filtering pass can tighten the list at added cost. The paper timed neighbor search rather than a full force/integration step and cautioned that package overhead reduces end-to-end gains.

A materialized list needs O(Nk) entries for mean neighbor count k, plus offsets and counts. Fixed-width rows sized to the largest particle's neighbors waste memory when one species has a much larger cutoff: the 2016 paper notes a 10:1 cutoff-radius disparity can mean roughly 1,000× more neighbors for large particles. Its implementation used per-type capacity and prefix-sum offsets instead. Implementations need explicit overflow handling (detect, resize and rebuild, or fail safely); silent truncation breaks completeness. Cell storage also needs occupancy capacity or compaction. The binary BVH has 2N−1 nodes in the 2019 construction, plus sort buffers and output list.

Both studies benchmark cubic periodic boxes. The quantized BVH traverses up to 27 translated query volumes in 3-D and first skips images that miss the root; cell stencils must avoid duplicate wrapped cells when spanning the box. These details matter when cutoff approaches a short box dimension. Neither study measures triclinic boxes. Axis-aligned bounds around skewed geometry may be looser and produce more false positives; this is an inference requiring a separate benchmark. Report box shape, periodicity, box-length/cutoff ratio, and image/duplicate policy.

## Verlet reuse and correctness

A Verlet list stores candidates to outer radius r_l=r_c+skin and reuses them, trading extra per-step force checks and list memory for fewer builds. A conservative deterministic rule rebuilds before any particle moves more than half the skin from its reference position, since two particles can approach; implementations must also account for periodic wrapping and box deformation. Larger skin or less frequent rebuilding can reduce build cost but increases candidate work and risks omitting a pair that enters r_c between rebuilds. The best point depends on velocity, timestep, force cost and system dynamics; measure amortized trajectory time, not only list-build latency.

Kim, Fábián and Hummer traced GROMACS artifacts to pairs that moved from outside an insufficient outer cutoff into the force cutoff between updates. Missed forces produced pressure-tensor errors and unphysical anisotropic deformation in large membranes; related effects appeared in atomistic and coarse-grained water, while dynamic pruning also caused pressure oscillations. This shows that long update intervals and short skins can bias observables even when individual steps appear stable. Choose skin and update interval against an explicit force, pressure or drift tolerance, check missed-pair diagnostics, and include fast particles and barostat-driven box changes. Probabilistic buffer estimates are not guarantees for all trajectories. [Kim, Fábián & Hummer 2023](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3553453_2/component/file_3575843/content)

## Practical reading

Cells are the low-build-cost baseline for one cutoff and even occupancy; stencils extend them to multiple cutoff classes while visits remain cheap. BVHs merit comparison for sparse long-range species, clustering, or cutoffs that leave grids mostly empty, but sum construction and traversal. Direct fused all-pairs merits a small-N/dense control although these papers do not measure it. On target hardware, compare candidate count, output bytes/overflow, build plus query, force time and amortized trajectory time. Keep correctness separate: no missed pairs inside r_c and acceptable observable error over the selected list lifetime.
