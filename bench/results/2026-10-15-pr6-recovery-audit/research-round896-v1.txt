## What the available properties can separate

| Hypothesis | Properties that can discriminate | Counterexample / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Device-memory or cache pressure | In ComputeBasic: `GPU_MEMORY_BYTE_READ/WRITE` (bytes), their `*_RATE` (GBpS), `L3_HIT/MISS`, `L3_READ/WRITE`, `L3_STALL`, `GPU_MEMORY_L3_READ/WRITE`, `GPU_MEMORY_REQUEST_QUEUE_FULL`, plus `LOAD_STORE_CACHE_*`. In time-based `MemoryProfile` and `DeviceCacheProfile`: 32/64-byte local-memory transactions, `L3_BUSY`, `L3_SUPERQ_FULL`, input/output-ready and LSC availability. | High traffic can be efficient streaming; low hit rate can be intentional bypass; high `L3_STALL` or queue-full time can be a consequence of another bottleneck. A hit percentage alone cannot prove cache latency or capacity is limiting. |
| Vector arithmetic / issue | `XVE_INST_EXECUTED_ALU0/1/2_ALL`, `XVE_INST_ISSUED_ALL`, `XVE_ACTIVE`, per-pipe utilization and `XVE_MULTIPLE_PIPE_ACTIVE`. These describe vector-engine issue/slots/activity, not FLOPs. | ALU slot events are not operation counts: vector width, instruction mix, predication and FMA semantics are not given; ALU1 excludes extended math. No selected metric identifies XMX activity. The B570's XMX count does not license mapping ALU0/1/2 to XMX. |
| SLM use/conflict | `SLM_BYTE_READ/WRITE` and `SLM_BANK_CONFLICT_COUNT`; `MemoryProfile` additionally exposes `SLM_ACCESS_COUNT` and XVE SLM read/write, atomic and fence messages. Pair counts with duration and work, not in isolation. | Conflict count lacks a denominator in ComputeBasic: many conflicts may be cheap amid huge useful traffic, while few conflicts can be costly in a tiny kernel. SLM bytes show use, not bank throughput or stall time. |
| Dispatch, occupancy, dependency/scheduling | `GPGPU_THREADGROUP_COUNT`, `GPGPU_DISPATCH`, compute-command busy/dispatch count, `XVE_THREADS_OCCUPANCY_ALL`, `XVE_STALL`; time-based `VectorEngineStalls` adds queue-active/stall, threadgroup resource-stall (including SLM/barrier/resource availability), barrier, scoreboard and send-wait reasons. | Low occupancy or active time can come from too little exposed work, long dependencies, resource limits or a small kernel. `XVE_STALL` only means a loaded thread has no pipe active; it does not name the cause. |
| Report acceptance and comparability | `ResultUncertainty`, `GpuTime`, `QueryBeginTime`, `CoreFrequencyMHz`/`AvgGpuCoreFrequencyMHz`, `ReportsCount`, and validity/scope flags. | Schema availability is not collection. An otherwise plausible percentage or count from a lost, mixed-context or split report cannot support a per-QSA claim. |
| Reject compromised reports | `ReportError`, `ReportLost`, `ReportInconsistent`, `ReportContextMismatch`, `ReportQueryModeMismatch`, `OverrunOccured`; also `ReportWithoutWorkload` and `ReportCtxSwitchLost`. | Reject for resource attribution: local descriptions say query error means ignore, begin/end trigger missing, context IDs inconsistent/different, query mode differs, the OA buffer overran, workload is absent, or other-context activity was not filtered. |
| Interpret scope or operating-state metadata | `QuerySplitOccurred`, `CoreFrequencyChanged`, `CoreFrequencyMHz`, `AvgGpuCoreFrequencyMHz`; `MidQueryTimer`, `MidQueryProgramming`, `MidQueryMarker`, `MidQueryCtxSwitch`, `MidQueryC6`, `MidQueryFreqChange`, `MidQueryMmioTrigger`. | Preserve these per report; split changes observation scope, while frequency and mid-query events call for grouping/exclusion rules in a stable comparison. They are not the same as `ReportError` and are not automatic universal invalidations. |
| Bound uncertainty | `ResultUncertainty` (percent). | Record it, but the selected property description gives no formula or threshold. Do not invent a pass percentage; require repeated valid samples and report spread. |

The three related time-based groups were also present in the closed catalog: `MemoryProfile` group 8 (69 metrics), `DeviceCacheProfile` group 6 (44), and `VectorEngineStalls` group 16 (40). They can refine a hypothesis if Main independently qualifies their query mode, but they are not part of the selected 83-column EVENT_BASED group. The group properties say those sets are time-based. Do not combine their time percentages with ComputeBasic event counts as if they came from one interval or one collection pass without verifying report identity and scope.

The denominators differ. `XVE_ACTIVE` is the time at least one vector pipe is active; occupancy is thread-slot occupancy; per-pipe utilization is time taken by that pipe; `XVE_STALL` counts a distinct condition. Stall-reason percentages in VectorEngineStalls may overlap in a cycle, per their descriptions, so do not add them or assume complements sum to 100%. They are not all normalized by the same resource or necessarily by whole-kernel elapsed time. A high active percentage does not mean peak device throughput; low occupancy does not by itself mean an occupancy-limited kernel.

For memory, use byte counters as physical device-local memory traffic only according to the driver description; they are not logical tensor bytes. `L3_READ/WRITE` are 64-byte request events, not bytes already transferred to VRAM; a first-order request-byte estimate is count × 64 only when the report confirms those counters cover the same query and no alternate granularity applies. `GPU_MEMORY_L3_READ` is described as GTI reads caused by L3 misses. Cache hit/miss counts may support a ratio only when both counters have the same event scope; even then the ratio is not transaction efficiency, useful-byte efficiency, or proof of a bottleneck. The reported `GBpS` unit identifies throughput, but the catalog does not expose its normalization equation or decimal/binary convention. Do not assume read-rate plus write-rate equals a validated aggregate until the same denominator and interval are verified. A conservative aggregate over disjoint reports is total bytes / total valid GPU-time, not the arithmetic mean of per-kernel rates; do not double-count concurrent intervals.

`GpuTime` is described as time elapsed on the GPU during the measurement, while `QueryBeginTime` is a measurement-begin timestamp. Neither is host API wall time or automatically the critical path through a multi-kernel dependency chain. A timestamp needs a clock-domain bridge before comparison with host time. The B570's advertised 380 GB/s and external PCIe links are specification/topology ceilings, not attained bandwidth. Event-query setup, instrumentation and possible serialization can change timing; the enumeration does not quantify that effect. Keep clean timing in separate, otherwise matched owned runs. Intel's profiling guide confirms hardware-counter collection is a separate analysis capability, not a timing-neutral guarantee. [GPU profiling tools guide](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/oneapi/optimization-guide-gpu/2024-2/performance-tools-in-intel-profiling-tools.html).

Do not convert `XVE_INST_*` event counts into FLOP/s: `ALU*_ALL` counts execution slots, and `XVE_INST_ISSUED_ALL` counts decoded instructions. They lack the executed operation, active-lane, vector-width and arithmetic semantics needed for conversion. In particular, the schema does not expose XMX utilization or an XMX operation counter. Legacy Intel GPA examples may help explain old-generation vocabulary, but the documentation itself notes terminology shifts on Arc and newer devices; an old Gen9 definition or threshold is not proof of identical Xe2 semantics. [Intel GPA GPU metrics reference](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/gpa/user-guide/2024-1/gpu-metrics.html).

## Validity and interpretation gates

The event group is a driver-supplied combination of 83 columns, but the catalog proves only property enumeration. Until Main resolves the independent status-loss gate and confirms a real query report, none of the above is available as measured QSA evidence. Also record query identity, selected group/mode, kernel/workload boundaries, report ID/count, query split, frequency, other-context activity and any concurrent GPU activity. Shared-device percentages can include work beyond the intended QSA component if context filtering is lost or scope differs. The captured envelope's uncovered gaps are not proof of hardware idle; no metric here establishes GPU saturation, health, or a causal bottleneck. Do not infer one from a high percentage alone.

## Decision support and next small steps

Two plausible patterns are separable after collection is qualified. **Memory/cache-limited candidate:** high sustained local-memory bytes per useful QSA work and queue/bank pressure, with measured valid time, would support testing a matched working-set/reuse change; counterexample is a well-utilized streaming path whose duration is set by compute or dependencies. **Issue/parallelism candidate:** high vector-pipe utilization with modest local-memory traffic would support an arithmetic/issue hypothesis, while low dispatch/occupancy and queue/resource/scoreboard stalls would favor insufficient parallel work or dependency/scheduling; counterexample is ALU activity overlapping a memory bottleneck, and XMX use remains unobserved. SLM conflict counts refine either interpretation but alone establish neither.

Main-owned follow-up should first close the collection gate, then capture a minimal representative QSA kernel interval with the selected ComputeBasic group and all validity metadata. Keep counter collection and clean speed timing separate, preserve per-run results and actual query boundaries, and compare repeated runs of one controlled component variation only after the first report passes validity review. If the event group does not activate or splits frequently, use a smaller supported set/report or a time-based companion group only under Main's existing qualification path. No universal counter thresholds or tuning choice follows from this enumeration.
