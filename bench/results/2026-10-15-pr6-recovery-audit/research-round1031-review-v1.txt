# R1031 — QSA head-parallel report review

Reviewer: native Codex, requested gpt-6-luna max. Classification: decision support. Read-only review; no source, build, test, GPU, process, or Git operations were performed.

## Assessment

R1030's central source reasoning is sound. For each head, the candidate forms the old lane contributions `p0..p3`; `(p0+p2)+(p1+p3)` is the old XOR16 then XOR8 root, and XOR4/2/1 finish the same tree. It selects lane roots 0/8/16/24 and accumulates heads in baseline order. The query-row and step guards are scalar and uniform across the 32-lane group; all lanes that continue reach each subgroup operation. With `active>0`, `active<=blocks`, and the reported validated spans, the eager key indices, dead-key indices, query/step reads, and lane-0 score stores stay inside their declared extents. No confirmed score-mapping, convergence, or in-range-address defect was found.

This is conditional IEEE-operation reasoning, not an empirical claim for every input. Under separate FP32 multiply/adds, preserved grouping, and the same device FP environment, the corresponding roots use the same operands and operation order; this supports bit identity for finite operands, including signed zeros and intermediate overflow/Inf. The R1030 fixture uses seeded finite values and exact full-score/full-ID comparisons; it does not specifically target negative zero, subnormals, cancellation-heavy vectors, overflow/Inf, or NaNs. Thus those classes remain source-level deductions. NaN payload/signaling identity across compilers is not established. Synthetic component agreement does not establish model quality.

## Material corrections and findings

**R1031-C1 — narrow the compiler-evidence gap.** R1030 says no candidate build command was available to verify contraction or relaxed-math settings. The separate `qsa-score-headparallel-build-v1.json` receipt records a successful `-O3 -fno-fast-math -ffp-contract=off -fsycl` compile/link and pins the resulting binary to the cohort. This confirms the recorded flags, but supplies no retained device IR/disassembly or optimizer proof for the reduction expression. Its `GPU_executed` field is false for the build phase; the later cohort is the execution evidence. Preserve the report's compiler-preservation caveat, while correcting the missing-command statement.

**R1031-C2 — retain the private-entry input/API contract.** The candidate requires `active>0`; the existing public scorer accepts its default `active=-1`. It also reads the full active pooled prefix before checking whether any query row uses that block. Since `active<=blocks`, these reads are in range, but the caller must initialize and keep `[0,active)` immutable until the queued work completes. The R1029 owner receipt proves allocation/zeroing and queue order for the existing public layer-major path, not that this still-private candidate has been wired to that caller. Before exposing or dispatching it, either preserve the public default or keep the entry private with a positive-active contract, and confirm/document the actual caller's pool and lifetime guarantees. This is a contract decision, not a demonstrated current-engine fault.

**R1031-C3 — make the repeated key loads explicit in the performance record.** `common[4][4]` loads sixteen pooled values per work-item. The four head groups repeat the same pooled-row values, producing four times as many logical pooled element references per work-group as the existing one-key-vector-per-lane scorer. Cache/coalescing may reduce physical traffic; source alone cannot say how much. The cohort itself reports logical key loads and physical traffic/register pressure as limits. Main's in-progress first-dispatch cache/CPU/UR evidence is the right next evidence before folding the candidate into the engine route.

The receipt says `production_selected=false` and `model_PP_measured=false`; it does not qualify full-model throughput/quality, physical traffic, RESTORE, or a production default. Keep those claims separate.

The report is substantive and specific to the head-parallel root mapping. Overlap with R1025 is low: that review concerns SLM staging and its barriers. R1029 overlaps on eager pooled reads, active-prefix ownership, and the boundary between entry and model dispatch; the current report adds a different private mapping, the exact root-tree derivation, and its four-head repeated loads. The R1029 generic caller caveat remains applicable, but its owner proof must not be silently promoted to candidate integration proof. No new researcher is eligible: Main's source/API integration review and current instrumented run can settle the remaining questions. Do not commission another survey unless those direct checks leave a concrete question they cannot answer.
