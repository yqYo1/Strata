# R854 — GPU SpTRSV dependency readiness and row scheduling

**Classification:** knowledge collection. **Scope:** non-LLM GPU sparse triangular solve (SpTRSV), with the coefficient matrix kept in the same CSR representation across scheduling arms. Literature results are reported as published; the scheduler comparisons below are not a cross-paper benchmark.

## Scope and source provenance

For a lower triangular system, row `i` becomes runnable after its off-diagonal predecessors produce their solution values. Let `G=(V,E)` be this DAG, `n=|V|`, `z=|E|`, `L` its level depth, `w_i` row work, and `R` the RHS count. Level scheduling materializes a topological partition. Counter scheduling updates remaining predecessor counts and publishes newly ready rows. Hybrid schedules spend some analysis to choose row granularity or group work.

The official NVIDIA CUDA Toolkit 12.6.2 cuSPARSE guide exposes `SpSV` analysis/solve and multi-RHS `SpSM`, opaque descriptors and workspace proportional to nonzero count; it does not disclose the default scheduler. The 2016 synchronization-free paper describes cuSPARSE as closed source, so its results on cuSPARSE 7.5 or 8.0 do not identify cuSPARSE 12.6.2 internals. The official rocSPARSE generic API page identifies release 5.0.0: `spsv` and `spsm` use buffer-size, preprocess and compute stages, and preprocessing can be reused across RHS. The `spsm` API accepts multiple RHS; internal row-order conversion may add copies/workspace. The rocSPARSE project `LICENSE.md` on mutable `develop` states MIT. These are API/provenance references, not schedule evidence; no vendor implementation code was copied. [NVIDIA cuSPARSE 12.6.2 API guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/12.6.2/cusparse/index.html); [rocSPARSE 5.0.0 generic API](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/rocSPARSE/en/latest/reference/generic.html); [rocSPARSE project license](https://github.com/ROCm/rocSPARSE/blob/develop/LICENSE.md).

The original 2016 Liu et al. paper presents a CUDA/OpenCL counter-based solver, measured with Tesla K40c, GTX Titan X and Radeon R9 Fury X. The 2021 Zhang et al. YuenyeungSpTRSV paper measures CUDA/OpenCL on GTX 1080, V100, RTX 2080 Ti and Radeon Vega 11. The 2023 Freire et al. Journal of Supercomputing paper reports analysis-assisted row grouping on RTX 2080 Ti and RTX 3090 Ti. Liu's method uses CSC successor traversal while Yuenyeung uses CSR, so their published results are not a format-controlled scheduler comparison. The papers are copyrighted research publications; this report paraphrases and adapts no code. Freire et al. state source release as future work; no pinned implementation is cited here. [Liu et al., “A Synchronization-Free Algorithm” (2016)](https://www.ssslab.cn/assets/papers/2016-liu-sptrsv.pdf); [Zhang et al., “YuenyeungSpTRSV” (2021)](https://www.ssslab.cn/assets/papers/2021-zhang-sptrsv.pdf); [Freire et al., “A GPU method for the analysis stage” (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11227-023-05238-8).

## Three schedules on a fixed CSR matrix

The comparison below fixes the CSR coefficient arrays and per-row arithmetic. A scheduler may build extra metadata, but its construction and storage count toward that schedule. This keeps matrix representation constant while exposing DAG readiness choices.

| Schedule | Readiness and work assignment | Best fit | Main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Level rows with level barriers** | Precompute `level[i]`, row lists and level offsets. Dispatch each level, then wait for all its rows. | Broad, shallow DAGs amortize each barrier across many rows. Repeated solves amortize level analysis. Row-length buckets can improve balance within a level. | Narrow/deep DAGs pay `L` small barriers/launches. A long row delays its level; compaction/preprocessing can dominate one-off solves. |
| **Dependency counter and ready frontier** | Initialize `pending[i]` and enqueue roots. On row completion, notify successors; the counter reaching zero publishes a ready row to workers. | Irregular widths and variable row work, when new rows can run before a full level drains. A queue avoids resident warps spinning on unready rows. | Dependency atomics contend at fan-in/fan-out; simultaneous releases contend at queue insertion/claim. A narrow critical path remains serial. |
| **Hybrid row granularity with dependency readiness** | Retain row-ready notifications, but use several rows per warp for short rows and a warp/group for long rows. Analyze row lengths or segments to choose the mapping. | Broad levels with many short rows waste lanes under one-warp-per-row, while long rows need more lanes. Can keep CSR and avoid a full level schedule. | Threshold/segment metadata adds preprocessing; mixed paths complicate progress and deadlock reasoning. Coarse averages misclassify skewed rows. |

Liu et al.'s counter algorithm is *not* an explicit queue: rows are assigned in parallel and spin on in-degree counters. A true ready frontier changes progress and contention. With CSR coefficient arrays fixed, the queue arm needs a successor index (row IDs grouped by predecessor), or must pay for rescanning rows. Building `succ_ptr[n+1]` and `succ_rows[z]` from CSR is scheduler metadata, not matrix-format conversion; charge its build and storage.

YuenyeungSpTRSV is a concrete hybrid: it handles short rows at thread level and longer rows at warp level, deriving segments from CSR row pointers while preserving row dependencies with per-component ready flags. The paper reports 5.98x over the compared synchronization-free baseline and 4.83x over cuSPARSE for matrices with parallel-granularity above 0.7, across four older GPU/APU platforms and a 245-matrix SuiteSparse set. This supports row-granularity adaptation for high-width, short-row graphs; it does not compare explicit ready queues with levels. The paper also identifies deadlock risk when thread-level and warp-level work mix carelessly under lock-step execution.

## Readiness visibility, ordering and numerical effects

A dependency count reaching zero is not by itself a safe publication protocol. A producer must finish writing the predecessor solution (and any partial row contribution) before publishing completion. Consumers must observe that publication with a memory order and scope that covers all participating GPU workgroups; a counter decrement and flag poll must not expose “ready” while data remains invisible. The 2021 Yuenyeung algorithm writes `x[i]`, executes CUDA `__threadfence()`, then sets `get_value[i]`; consumers wait for the flag before reading `x`. This is evidence of one CUDA design, not a portable recipe. On another backend, use its documented device-scope release/acquire atomic or equivalent fence protocol, verify that flag accesses are race-free, and confirm queue progress guarantees. A global workgroup barrier cannot be assumed inside an ordinary multi-block kernel. Queue publication itself also needs ordered record writes before the tail/ready marker becomes visible.

Level ordering does not automatically make floating-point results reproducible: rows may be independent within a level, and downstream sums can still arrive in a different order if implemented as scatter atomics. Conversely, a row gather with fixed predecessor traversal can keep an ordered local reduction even with a dynamic row schedule. Counter/queue execution can change row completion and scatter accumulation order, yielding different roundoff or nondeterministic low bits. For multiple RHS, readiness belongs to the row, not to each scalar RHS. Solving a tile of RHS together can amortize dependency/queue events and reuse row values, but increases live partial state roughly with `n*R` and per-row register pressure. The `SpSM` APIs establish that multi-RHS solves exist; they do not establish that any cited SpTRSV schedule scales linearly or wins at a particular `R`.

## Break-even model and observed evidence

Use a deliberately simple full-path model. Let `P_s` be schedule `s`'s one-time preprocessing cost, `S_s(R)` its per-solve cost, and `K` the number of solves over an unchanged sparsity pattern. Then `T_s(K,R)=P_s+K*S_s(R)`; if `S_A<S_B`, A pays off after `K > (P_A-P_B)/(S_B-S_A)`. A level-set `P_L` includes dependency-depth analysis and row-list compaction; `S_L` is row work plus imbalance and approximately `L-1` global barriers (or launches). A counter/queue `P_Q` includes successor-list construction and initialization; `S_Q` includes row work, about `z` dependency notifications, `n` row claims/queue publications in the simple design, queue contention, and the longest dependency path. A hybrid adds threshold/segment construction and per-solve dispatch; it should be charged for both dynamic synchronization and the metadata it consumes. All terms depend on hardware and whether preprocessing is repeated after value-only versus pattern changes.

On fixed CSR, the base matrix bytes are unchanged. Typical level metadata is `level[n]` plus `level_ptr[L+1]` and `row_ids[n]` (roughly 8n+4L bytes for 32-bit indices, excluding temporary scan buffers). A counter queue with CSR-derived successors adds about `4z + 8n` bytes for 32-bit successor IDs/pointers, plus `pending[n]`, a ready queue up to `n` row IDs, and partial accumulators. These illustrative sizes assume 32-bit row IDs/counters and no alignment; report actual bytes. Per solve, counters, queue state and partial sums must be reset or initialized; level lists may be reused unchanged. Multiple RHS may make partial storage O(nR), while a queue can still schedule rows once per solve rather than once per RHS.

Published counterevidence rejects a universal “dynamic always wins” claim. Liu et al. measured a level-set implementation on dual-socket Intel E5-2695 v3 CPUs: `FEM/ship003` had 4,367 levels and barrier synchronization was 85% of the solve; `nlpkkt160` had only two levels, where barrier cost was negligible. In their GPU comparison, synchronization-free beat cuSPARSE v7.5 on Titan X by 2.3x single precision and 2.14x double on average across 11 matrices, but was only comparable on `nlpkkt160`; atomics were central to their approach. This does not prove a queue beats levels, and CPU barrier fractions are not GPU timings. The 2023 analysis-assisted hybrid study found the row-grouped solver's preprocessing break-even generally at 2–19 solves, but over 100 for `road_usa` and `nlpkkt160`; for five matrices per platform, the plain solve's time was below the hybrid analysis cost. Thus a simple no-analysis solver can win when reuse is low or rows-per-category are few. These published values use different matrices, algorithms and GPUs; no direct ranking is justified.

## One future experiment design

Use the SuiteSparse `FEM/ship003` lower-triangular factor plus diagonal, retaining the 121,728-row, 8,086,034-nonzero structure and 4,367-level shape reported in Liu et al. Freeze one CSR coefficient array and RHS data for all arms. Compare (1) level rows with level barriers, (2) dependency-counter frontier with a CSR-derived successor list and bounded queue batches, and (3) the same frontier with thread/warp row-length grouping. Run `R=1,8,32` RHS and `K=1,16,128` solves without changing the pattern; time preprocessing separately and include it in cumulative time. Record rows/level width, row-length percentiles, successor fan-in/fan-out, queue high-water mark, atomic serialization, per-level barrier wait, row-work imbalance and total solve critical-path time. Validate residuals and per-row numerical differences across repetitions, preserving a deterministic row-reduction order where an arm claims it. The decisive result is the measured `K` crossover and whether the frontier reduces barrier delay without queue and atomic costs dominating at this narrow/deep point; old hardware results do not predict it.

## Limits

These sources establish mechanisms and historical hardware behavior, not performance on a current GPU or a particular vendor release. API analysis stages are opaque and may use different internals between releases. Readiness primitives, progress, memory scopes, atomics and queue implementation are backend-specific. The comparisons deliberately hold CSR matrix storage and arithmetic fixed; any successor list or level structure remains scheduler overhead, not a format conversion. No local matrix, build, GPU, or profiler run was performed, and no Strata change is proposed.
