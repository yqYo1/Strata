# R262: attained capacity versus workload amount

## Decision

Changing submission order can improve overlap only when the engine’s dependencies permit it and an uninstrumented timeline shows that the operations lie on the same critical path. It cannot reduce bytes, expert calls, or materializations. The safe two-queue control in the retained measurement found no intersection between 144 target copies and 3,168 GEMMs; that rules out claiming overlap for that control, but does not prove that all future scheduling is unable to overlap.

The measured component rates show where the headroom is. They do not by themselves establish current production latency: routed expert counts, generic-versus-fused branch counts, actual DMA bytes, allocations, and timeline dependencies are not yet joined for one workload. Where the conditional 32K arithmetic already uses 33.46 seconds for serialized packed-weight plus all-RAM residual transfers against a 32.768-second budget, reordering alone cannot meet that modeled budget without overlap; avoiding/reducing transfers or changing placement/retention is required. That is still a scenario, not a production miss. The selected architecture should follow a matched workload census and timeline, not these ceilings alone.

## Rates and workload counts

The measurements are from a Ryzen 5 5600X, 128 GB RAM, and Intel Arc B570. They report operation-specific attained rates: CPU RAM read 36.87 GB/s; non-temporal copy 37.58 GB/s counting logical read plus write; three-worker packed-copy pool 13.74 GB/s one-way payload; H2D Host USM 6.447 GB/s; pageable H2D/D2H 4.605/5.274 GB/s; VRAM logical copy 330.07 GB/s; GPU dense FP16-input/FP32-output GU/Down GEMM 52.69/41.61 TFLOP/s at 8,192 rows, falling to 13.16/10.08 TFLOP/s with 80 rows and eight rotating weights. These are distinct operations and conventions. Intel lists 380 GB/s as the B570 graphics-memory bandwidth; that vendor specification is not the attained copy rate. The measured external path is Gen4x4, whose 128/130-encoded ideal is 7.877 GB/s per direction before packet overhead; it is not a replacement for measured H2D.

The inspected current native pack has seven expert shapes across 48 layers, 24,576 experts total, and 50,292,326,400 packed bytes. Per-pair expert counts and bytes are: 18/20: 7,168 at 2,176,000 B each; 18/42: 1,536 at 1,715,200 B; 21/20: 4,608 at 2,329,600 B; 21/42: 512 at 1,868,800 B; 22/20: 7,680 at 1,971,200 B; 22/42: 2,560 at 1,510,400 B; 23/20: 512 at 2,662,400 B. The inspected header census proves MAXBLOB=2,662,400 B and the per-layer `MAXBLOB * 512` temporary-cache formula is 1,363,148,800 B. Neither number proves the runtime selected-pack/free peak or whether original cache/MTP allocations overlap.

For a hypothetical single copy of every unique packed expert, dividing 50.292 GB by the separate 6.447 GB/s Host-USM H2D rate gives 7.80 seconds. Historical expert-copy accounting was 190.241 GB, or 29.50 seconds at that rate (24.15 seconds at the ideal encoded link rate). The layer-major design’s all-experts-per-layer loading changes the byte count to one unique packed population, about 3.78 times fewer bytes than that historical estimate. Its cache is reused across prompt chunks, but generic dequantization still runs for each selected expert on each chunk. Packed-weight reuse therefore does not imply decoded-weight reuse.

The qualified GPU generic IQ expansion measurements are 76–208 microseconds per expert depending on the seven shape pairs. Each call materializes 9,830,400 B of FP16 GU+Down output, regardless of routed row count. Multiplying the per-pair medians by the full inspected population gives 2.826 seconds for one hypothetical all-expert pass, writing 241.592 GB and reading 50.292 GB of packed input (291.884 GB total logical traffic). This arithmetic uses the measured generic branch medians; it is not a current-workload estimate. Actual time is `sum(pair, chunk) expert_calls(pair, chunk) * measured_pair_time`. Do not apply generic rates to MMQ, fused, peer, or unknown branches. The 8K/80-row GEMM comparison also does not establish a production row histogram.

## 32K prompt budget scenarios

At 1,000 input tokens/s, 32,768 positions allow 32.768 seconds total. The corrected residual stream width is D=10,240 FP32 elements per position (40,960 B/position), across 47 layer boundaries. If all residual handoffs are in RAM, the source-derived conditional payload is 63.082 GB H2D plus 63.082 GB D2H; at the measured pageable rates that is 25.659 seconds. Add one unique packed-weight pass (7.801 seconds at Host-USM rate) for 33.460 seconds, before GEMM or any other work. This exceeds the budget by 0.692 seconds if serialized. It requires fewer bytes, a different placement/retention scheme, or demonstrated overlap to fit this arithmetic. These are conditional copy-rate estimates, not measured DMA time or proof that the production path uses RAM handoffs.

A hypothetical effective 8,192-position GPU residual prefix has 320 MiB backing and yields 19.244 seconds residual handoff plus 7.801 seconds packed weights, or 27.237 seconds if non-inplace D2D work is included; in-place aliasing removes the modeled D2D transfers, giving 27.045 seconds. For 16,384, 24,576, and 32,768 GPU positions, the conditional non-inplace totals are 21.013, 14.789, and 8.566 seconds; in-place totals are 20.631, 14.216, and 7.801 seconds. These arithmetic scenarios do not qualify numerical behavior, peak fit, or overlap. The current layer-major source also requires resident QSA K/V. At 262,144 positions, residual alone is 10 GiB, 71,303,168 B larger than the entire 10,666,115,072 B device capacity, before QSA/KV or other allocations. Reordering cannot repair that capacity contradiction; memory footprint or placement must change. Cache/MTP release-and-restore also has graph-retirement and queue-drain ordering requirements, so arbitrary reordering is not safe.

## What evidence would decide

For one matched ≥32K prompt, record by layer/chunk/pair: routed expert calls and rows, chosen dequant/GEMM branch, packed bytes copied, output bytes materialized, residual bytes and direction, and each operation’s start/end plus queue/dependency. Join those counts to the corresponding operation-specific measurements. Then separate (a) time proven hidden under useful work, (b) exposed critical-path service, and (c) total bytes/work that no schedule can remove. If exposed service dominates, scheduling/parallelism may help; if the byte/call total itself uses the budget, reduce repeated copies/materialization or change residency/reuse. Qualify full 262,144-position allocation lifetime/peak before making a fit claim. No architecture is adopted by this analysis.

## Method limits and references

The Roofline model relates arithmetic intensity to compute and memory ceilings; it helps distinguish potential compute-bound from bandwidth-bound kernels. It does not establish this workload’s measured traffic, cache behavior, DMA overlap, or dependency critical path.
