# R552 — QSA indexer storage precision and quality evidence

Date: 2026-10-15 JST. Read-only source and primary-reference review of pooled QSA indexer key and transient indexer-query storage. This is separate from recurrent GDN state, ordinary attention K/V, R550's score/address budget, and R551's launch mapping. No source, test, benchmark, or runtime setting was changed. No build/test, GPU, profiler, process/service, reset, cleanup, Git/live/private/model record or excluded `B/prefill-gemm-only-contract-root-v1/record.json` was inspected. Main owns any implementation and serial validation.

## Finding

FP16 or BF16 storage for the *transformed pooled indexer keys* is a plausible memory-capacity experiment: at the configured 262,144-cell endpoint, the source allocates 384 MiB of FP32 pooled-key capacity across 12 QSA layers. Two-byte storage would save about 192 MiB at that maximum, if all associated writers, readers, snapshots, and graph paths are correctly converted. At the 32,768-cell endpoint the corresponding saving is about 24 MiB. This is a configured capacity reduction, not a measured reduction in peak process/device memory or a speedup.

The precision change is not validated. It may change selector scores and selected cells; exact top-k only guarantees the selected IDs are exact for the scores it receives. Existing evidence supports a carefully controlled trial, but does not establish no quality loss at 262k context or on Strata's SYCL hardware. Trial FP16 and BF16 independently, retaining FP32 score accumulation and exact top-k, and judge them on paired downstream task quality as well as selector/output diagnostics. Do not reject a candidate merely because some selected IDs flip, and do not accept it from needle retrieval alone.

## What the current source stores and computes

The loaded Qwen geometry is `idx_dim=128`, `idx_block=4`, `idx_n_head=4`, `idx_top_k=2048`, with 12 QSA and 36 GDN layers. `QsaState::idx_pooled` is `float*`; capacity is `max_cells/idx_block + 2` rows, each with 128 FP32 values. `qsa_state_bytes` includes that pooled-key buffer in every QSA layer. The extra two rows are included in the estimates below; the total does not include allocator alignment or unrelated session state.

| Configured max cells | Pooled rows per QSA layer | FP32 pooled-key capacity, 12 QSA layers | Two-byte pooled-key capacity | Nominal saving |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32,768 | 8,194 | 48 MiB + 12 KiB | 24 MiB + 6 KiB | 24 MiB + 6 KiB |
| 65,536 | 16,386 | 96 MiB + 12 KiB | 48 MiB + 6 KiB | 48 MiB + 6 KiB |
| 262,144 | 65,538 | 384 MiB + 12 KiB | 192 MiB + 6 KiB | 192 MiB + 6 KiB |

The equation is `12 * (max_cells/4 + 2) * 128 * bytes_per_element`. “Two-byte” applies equally to FP16 and BF16 capacity; it says nothing about score error. The separate raw indexer tail is small: three 128-float vectors per layer plus a 128-float dead vector, and incoming tail values are already rounded to FP16 before storing. Do not count tail savings as part of the pooled-capacity headline.

Verifier query storage is transient scratch, shared across layers, not a 12-layer cache. `qidx_` is `T * IQ * ID` FP32 values, with `IQ=4`, `ID=128`, or 2,048 bytes/query. At maximum verifier window `T=4`, that is 8 KiB total; a two-byte query buffer would save at most 4 KiB. Converting query storage may add cast/conversion work and can change rankings independently of key storage. Test key-only, query-only, and combined variants separately so their effects remain attributable.

The existing write/read path matters. Prefill's `native_qsa_indexer_append_batch` rounds raw incoming index-key tail values to F16, pools each four-token group, normalizes and applies RoPE, then writes the transformed pooled key into FP32 `idx_pooled`. The native decode/verifier append path documents the same F16 raw-tail cache/F32 arithmetic arrangement and writes transformed pooled keys as FP32. The verifier query projection uses BF16 weights with FP32 GEMV output; RMSNorm and RoPE produce FP32 `qidx_`. The QSA selector consumes these pooled keys and queries with FP32 products/accumulation; ReLU and head reduction feed FP32 score storage. The root top-k chooses the exact highest weighted candidates for those computed scores and emits sorted IDs. Therefore post-transform key/query storage casting followed by FP32 score accumulation is a distinct candidate from lowering the arithmetic precision or changing the selection algorithm.

An existing `STRATA_IDX_FP16_CHECK` debug hook in prefill copies pooled keys and the dead vector rounded to FP16, reruns prefill selection with the FP32 score routine, and prints cumulative identical-selection count and cell overlap. This is useful as a narrow diagnostic precedent only: it is opt-in and was not run here; it covers FP16 keys, not BF16 or query casting, only the instrumented prefill path, and only selector IDs/overlap. It allocates large temporary FP32 buffers, synchronizes/copies IDs, and has no end-to-end quality outcome. It cannot qualify a production storage change or stand in for a verifier/decode-path experiment.

## External evidence

| Evidence | What it supports | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8 architecture paper, [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30320) | QSA uses a compressed indexer to score/select context microblocks; indexer precision is part of the sparse-attention route. | Architecture description, not a post-training indexer-storage precision ablation. It does not establish FP16/BF16 no-harm. |
| DeepSeek-V3.2 report, [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02556), plus [official inference implementation](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp/blob/main/inference/model.py) | Closely related DSA implementation supports a quantized indexer path: normalized queries and compressed keys are quantized to FP8 for indexer dot products, and the indexer key cache includes scales. The report describes a model trained with indexer warmup and sparse-attention stages. | This is another model and a jointly trained path; its training and post-training recipe prevents the result from proving that post-hoc FP16/BF16 storage on Qwen3.8 is harmless. Its score arithmetic and hardware path also differ. |
| Merged vLLM PR [#54890](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/54890) for Qwen3.8 Flash-Next | Direct exact-model evidence that an optional FP8 E4M3 QSA indexer cache has been implemented. It stores normalized Q and compressed K in FP8 and computes indexer scores from those operands. Reported E2E BF16-versus-FP8 results include GSM8K 0.9682/0.9689 versus 0.9678/0.9685 (n=2,638), LongBench v2 0.4871 versus 0.5050 (n=503), matching reasoning traces, and mean prompt-logprob delta at most 0.008. The PR reports needle QA at 4.9k/19.5k/39k and GB300 speed measurements. This justifies an experiment on exact-model indexer precision. | These are PR-reported NVIDIA/GB300 measurements, not Strata or SYCL results. The task set and context lengths do not establish 262k no-harm. Benchmark scores vary and are not a formal equivalence test. The FP8 path also lowers score operand precision; it is not a pure low-bit storage / FP32 arithmetic test. |
The DeepSeek DSA examples make a lower-precision indexer technically credible, and the vLLM PR is unusually relevant because it measures Qwen3.8 itself. Neither demonstrates that Strata's proposed post-transform FP16 or BF16 cache with FP32 scoring is harmless. The Qwen3.8 evidence also concerns the indexer specifically; ordinary attention KV precision does not substitute for it. A citation to a successful FP8 system is evidence to test, not a transferable quality guarantee.

## Quality measurement and decision gate

Run FP16-key and BF16-key arms separately; if query storage is considered, add independent FP16-query and BF16-query arms, then combined arms only after attributing the individual effects. Keep baseline selection/scoring code, top-k ordering, downstream attention, weights and prompt set fixed. The candidate should cast only when writing the transformed pooled key (and, in the query arm, after query projection, normalization and RoPE); reads must convert to FP32 before the existing score arithmetic if the question is storage precision rather than lower-precision score math. Verify all prefill, one-token decode and multi-token verifier writers/readers, snapshot/restore, host/device copies, and capture/replay lifetimes before any broad run.

At each tested context, record selected-ID exact-match fraction, per-query Jaccard/recall against baseline, selected-set overlap, score differences, cutoff margin, and whether flips concentrate on low-margin candidates. These diagnose the mechanism; exact ID equality is not the product-quality oracle. Compare selected-attention outputs and full generated outputs/log-probabilities, then evaluate paired representative downstream tasks with matched prompt, seed/decoding and baseline repetitions. Include short and long contexts and the longest supported context; use task coverage that exercises actual reasoning, coding, instruction following, and long-context use. Needle retrieval can remain a narrow diagnostic, but cannot alone establish quality.

Predeclare a task-level non-inferiority margin and its one-sided confidence procedure. A small 24-task screen with 1 percentage-point margin is only a screen: even zero observed regressions leaves an approximate one-sided 95% upper bound of 11.73% on a binary harm rate. Roughly 299 independent no-harm pairs would be needed for that same zero-event bound to fall below 1%; multiple prompts from the same task or source are correlated and do not count as independent pairs. Report task-level paired effects and uncertainty, not only aggregate accuracy or selected-ID agreement. A real quality loss on a representative task is a rejection even if capacity savings are useful; failure to detect a loss on an underpowered sample is inconclusive, not acceptance.

Storage savings are materially larger for pooled keys than for the transient query. If memory capacity is the intended win, prioritize key-only arms and verify the saved allocation against the actual session plan. Keep kernel/runtime performance a separate outcome: FP16/BF16 conversion, cache traffic and selector speed may interact; this source review makes no speed claim. Main should decide adoption only after correctness, quality and target-hardware measurements pass their separately defined gates.
