## Prior decisions and what remains open

R289 separated GEMM output-storage casts (`GU`, `Dm`), GDN workspaces and recurrent state, and required local paired quality checks. It found no directly comparable public numerical quality result for these stores. R299 added recurrent-state studies and cautioned that model/format/update-specific results cannot qualify an uncalibrated cast. R291 proposed BF16 for a different path: a full host-backed inter-layer residual array in explicit `run_layer_major`; its `std::vector<float>` stores all prompt rows. That is not this integrated path's two-buffer stage handoff. R301 already bounded a possible 16-bit `Dm` store to the compact, single-GPU, non-MMQ fallback and showed why a generic conversion has to account for each writer, the FP32 combine, and shared-region maxima. The open question there is whether its old-source result applies to this integrated `@0691bcdf` tree and actual 8K/64K target configuration.

No published result found establishes a numeric “tiny” ability loss for a plain FP16/BF16 store on this exact model and engine. Qwen Team's own report says FP8 GR residual storage cuts bytes in half versus BF16 “with almost no loss,” but gives no numeric delta/protocol in that statement; it is relevant context for residual rows, not proof for this implementation or `Dm`. DAMP reports near-FP32 FP16 recurrent-state results on Qwen3.6-35B while BF16 varies by task; LeapQuant reports substantial losses for naïve per-token BF16 recurrent-state storage on Qwen3.5, and its successful 8-bit method includes windowing/buffering/compensation. These are recurrent feedback results from other checkpoints, not a prediction for stage handoff or `Dm`. They argue against asserting zero loss from dtype width alone. Primary sources: [Qwen Team report §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30320v1), [DAMP v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27513v2), [LeapQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38166v1). No SGLang/vLLM or vendor reference located in this screen quantifies quality for these exact persistent stores.

## Candidate A — stage-split residual handoff rows

This is the clearest new integrated-source gap. `Impl::hand[2]` is `float*` at line 489. In `init`, a split stage allocates two pinned host buffers of `chunk * D * 4` bytes at lines 693–696. After one stage finishes a chunk, line 2949 copies `m.R` (the four-branch residual rows) into the selected host buffer, waits for the compute stream at 2949–2951, then passes that buffer to the next stage at 2957–2959. The receiver copies the same rows into its FP32 compute buffer `m.R` at lines 1299–1303. Thus `hand` contains completed values, not a running accumulator: its producer is the completed-stage residual `m.R`; its sole consumer is the next stage's `m.R`; the buffer remains live until that asynchronous receiver read is complete, with two chunks ping-ponged. Any narrow-store probe must preserve the FP32 `m.R` compute representation and convert only at this handoff boundary.

For T=8,192 and D=10,240 floats/token, the two FP32 handoff buffers total 640 MiB pinned host storage. A BF16 or FP16 representation would use 320 MiB and save 320 MiB pinned host capacity; this does not spend against or release the stated 1,763 MiB VRAM headroom. At a 64K prompt in 8K chunks, payload across one stage boundary is 2.5 GiB per direction in FP32 and 1.25 GiB per direction in 16-bit; the byte opportunity is 1.25 GiB in each direction, 2.5 GiB combined per boundary. These are exact payload arithmetic from the source shape, not elapsed-time or throughput estimates. They apply only when the integrated layer-split path is active; ordinary single-stage prefill does not allocate `hand` (`next_ != nullptr` guard at lines 693–696).

The public Qwen report is the closest architectural evidence because this is GR residual state crossing a layer boundary, but its FP8 statement is qualitative and does not validate BF16/FP16 in Strata. Required local scope if pursued: first compare the same-source FP32 and 16-bit handoff path on identical teacher-forced token IDs at T=8,192 and the 64K target, with identical layer split/chunks, routing, weights and generation settings. Record each handoff's residual max/p99.9/RMS/relative error and nonfinite/overflow/underflow counts; compare post-prompt logits/top-k, paired continuation NLL/KL/top-1 at long-context positions, plus fixed code and retrieval outcomes. Establish the quality margin from control repeatability and the explicitly accepted task-loss limit before reading candidate results. The literature does not supply that limit or a target-model “zero loss” claim.

## Candidate B — MoE `Dm` expert-contribution plane

`Dm` is still an FP32 output plane at `Impl` line 433 and is allocated as `T*K*N` floats in `moe_set_bytes` lines 640–653 and in `carve` line 825. Down-product producers include grouped IQ / compact fallback (e.g. lines 2155, 2178–2205), MMQ paths (2468, 2754), and CPU sharing's FP32 copy (2858). `moe_combine` consumes it at line 2868, after all down outputs complete. Each expert row is a stored contribution; the FP32 weighted accumulation happens in `moe_combine`, not in `Dm`. The plane is live from each down-product write through that combine in one layer/chunk, then reused. The route-rank FMA accumulation and `bo` remain FP32 if storage is changed.

At T=8,192, K=10 and N=2,560, the raw Dm allocation is 800 MiB; a 16-bit plane is 400 MiB, so the absolute raw reduction is 400 MiB (22.7% of the stated 1,763 MiB headroom). That is not a proven 400 MiB arena saving: `carve` places Dm within `region = max(gdn_set_bytes, qsa_set_bytes, moe_set_bytes)` at lines 797–825. Unless MoE is the maximum before and after the change, some or all Dm shrinkage is hidden by the aliased GDN/QSA region. At 64K with T=8,192 the allocation is reused eight times, so the per-chunk peak remains 800 MiB / at most 400 MiB saved; do not multiply the peak saving by eight. FP32→16-bit packing and widening, conversion cost, and route-specific writers could offset throughput.

R301's narrow single-GPU compact non-MMQ fallback remains the only previously bounded implementation candidate; its source writer/reader map must be rechecked for this integrated tree. Generic Dm16 is still not a one-cast patch: the tree exposes multiple down writers and a float `moe_combine` reader. A narrow implementation would require an eligible single-GPU fallback condition, a proven temporary/alias lifetime for any FP32 GEMM output tile, 16-bit pack after FP32 accumulation, and a combine load that widens while preserving rank order and FP32 FMA. Until mode metadata confirms that branch and the arena maximum, report raw bytes only. R301's historical “shared max masks savings” conclusion should not be mechanically copied to this source because the current `region` is max of GDN/QSA/MoE and does not show PLE in this max expression.

Required local scope if pursued: exercise only a source-eligible fallback, compare every expert contribution before/after cast, combined `bo`, final prompt logits and paired 64K teacher-forced continuation NLL/KL/top-k. Include FP16 and BF16 as separate arms; record finite/overflow/underflow counts and per-layer error distributions. Add fixed code/retrieval checks and compare against repeated FP32-control noise. Do not treat recurrent-state paper scores or Qwen's residual-state sentence as quality evidence for `Dm`; no published Dm-storage result for this model was found.

## Selection

Keep both as conditional candidates, ordered by purpose. Candidate A is a new and directly source-proven host-pinned/PCIe boundary opportunity only for the stage-split route; it cannot solve VRAM headroom. Candidate B is the stronger possible VRAM candidate, but only 400 MiB raw at 8K and its actual arena saving is unresolved because of the max overlay and heterogeneous writers. Neither candidate is recommended for adoption without local paired quality tests and the exact active-mode capacity receipt. The one numerical result in other models is not a proof of zero loss for this target.
