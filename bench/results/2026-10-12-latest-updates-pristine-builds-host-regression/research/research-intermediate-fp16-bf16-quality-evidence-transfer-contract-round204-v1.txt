# Round 204 — intermediate precision quality evidence and transfer contract

Date: 2026-10-12 (Asia/Tokyo). Read-only source, closed-report/registry, and primary-paper review. No source edits, imports, tests, builds, runtime/model/GPU/process query, Git command, or measurement were performed. This report is literature/source evidence and a proposed comparison contract; it contains no new Strata quality or performance result.

## Finding

The blanket statement “FP32 recurrent state is necessary” is too strong. A direct published ablation on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B finds FP16 GDN recurrent-state storage close to FP32 on six reasoning/code benchmarks and on RULER through 128K; uniform BF16 is less stable on some reasoning/code scores. This is credible evidence that FP16 state storage can work in a closely related Qwen hybrid MoE with FP32 arithmetic, but it does not qualify Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, this Strata implementation, RTX B570, or full 262,144-cell context. The conclusion is specific to *persistent recurrent-state storage*: it does not establish safety of reducing every FP32 intermediate.

For the current fallback MoE, the SwiGLU bridge `Hh` is already FP16 (`uint16_t`) in both compact-on and compact-off modes. That toggle cannot answer whether FP32 `Hh` would improve quality. The benchmark needs separate arms for the true target (GDN state storage FP32 vs FP16) and, only if desired, the transient bridge (`Hh` FP16 vs a newly implemented FP32 counterfactual). Keep accumulation, logits, KV, and weight formats separately fixed and named.

## Primary measured quality evidence

Zhang et al., *DAMP: Decay-Aware Mixed-Precision Recurrent-State Quantization*, arXiv:2608.27513v2 (30 Sep 2026), evaluates recurrent-state formats in SGLang, using its default FP32 recurrent-state storage as reference. Its hybrid MoE Qwen3.6-35B-A3B has 30 GDN and 10 full-attention layers. Across AIME 2026, HMMT Feb 2026, IMO-AnswerBench, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, and LiveCodeBench-v6, the reported `FP32 / FP16 / BF16` scores are respectively: AIME `85.46 / 84.58 / 79.71`; HMMT `57.57 / 55.82 / 54.97`; IMO-Ans `50.29 / 49.42 / 43.71`; GPQA-D `81.97 / 82.13 / 81.66`; MMLU-Pro `84.66 / 84.61 / 84.65`; LCB-v6 `86.95 / 86.80 / 84.10`. These are the paper’s scores; it does not report uncertainty intervals in the cited table. Its generation setup uses thinking mode, temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20 and up to 65,536 output tokens for Qwen3.6; benchmark response counts vary (AIME 128, HMMT 64, IMO-Ans 16, GPQA-D 32, MMLU-Pro 16, LCB-v6 8 per problem).

The appendix’s long-context RULER macro accuracy compares the state format throughout prefill and decode. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B FP32 vs FP16 is `96.96/96.98` at 4K, `96.94/96.94` at 8K, `96.65/96.65` at 16K, `96.56/96.56` at 32K, `96.32/96.32` at 64K, and `96.13/96.11` at 128K. This directly addresses state storage and long-context task quality through 128K. It does not reach 256K, nor establish Strata’s chunked prefill or last-cell behavior. The paper also says prefill uses FP32 arithmetic within each chunk and writes its final state into the compressed cache; decode reconstructs state, performs the update/readout in FP32, and writes FP16/INT8 state. Thus it is a storage-roundtrip experiment, not “run all recurrent arithmetic in half.”

The same paper’s FP16/BF16 recurrent-state comparison on Qwen3.6 is more relevant than general mixed-precision training or weight-only quantization results. Results differ on another model (Kimi-K3 BF16 is close to FP32), showing model/config dependence. DAMP is an arXiv paper, not an independently reproduced Strata result. Its state-format results support testing an FP16 arm; they do not grant an unmeasured quality guarantee.

Source: [DAMP arXiv abstract/version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27513), [full paper, main results and setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27513#S6), [long-context results and implementation details](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27513#A4).

## What other evidence does and does not establish

- The DAMP Qwen3.6 ablation is direct for recurrent state. It is not direct for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next or transient MoE activations. DAMP also reports that INT8/FP8 recurrent-state quantization loses substantial math/code accuracy despite near-preservation on some general-reasoning tasks. That makes task coverage important; it does not imply FP16 is unsafe.
- *Low-Bit Recurrent States in Hybrid Language Models* (arXiv:2609.30950, submitted to ICASSP 2027) reports six-bit mean recurrent-state payload within 0.005 nats/token of FP32 on three hybrid models. It is an author preprint and concerns quantized persistent state, not transient activations. Treat it as corroboration only; it does not replace the exact model comparison.
- Mamba’s original and later mixed-precision discussions often concern training stability or kernel speed. Those cannot by themselves prove FP32 inference-state quality necessity. DAMP’s controlled state-format task results are more directly relevant to this model family.
- BF16 vs FP16 are not interchangeable: both use 16 bits, but their exponent/mantissa trade differs. In the Qwen3.6 recurrent-state table, FP16 is closer to FP32 than BF16 on math and code; do not infer that result for a different checkpoint or a different activation buffer.
- Weight quantization, activation quantization inside GEMMs, FP32-vs-FP16 accumulator choice, KV-cache quantization, and recurrent-state storage are different interventions. Results from one must not be cited as an answer to another.
- Exact output IDs or selected top-five log probabilities are diagnostic gates, not distributional/task-quality measures. Prior R168 explicitly limits its existing T=64/2049/32768/65536 ID/LP5 parity to narrow checks; it does not qualify full distributions or use of all 262,144 context cells.

## Current source dtype/lifetime map

Audited source snapshot: `/home/yayoi/ghq/github.com/yqYo1/Strata/.worktree/docs-sycl-storage-retention-20261009/src/prefill/prefill.cpp` and `include/strata/core/layer.hpp`, current code at report-registry-v153 publication lineage. This was source-only inspection.

- **Transient MoE gate/up output:** `m.GU` is `float*`, gate/up GEMM writes FP32. `swiglu_interleaved` computes the nonlinear result and stores it to `m.Hh` as `uint16_t` (FP16); the down GEMM reads `Hh` and writes `m.Dm` FP32. The route combiner and residual path use FP32. Compact scratch changes row packing/grouping; both compact branches share the FP16 bridge. Therefore compact ON/OFF is not an FP32-vs-FP16 activation comparison.
- **Router:** expert logits and route weights are FP32 (`m.logits`, `m.w`); route IDs are int32. Router rank/weight accumulation is not the `Hh` store.
- **Persistent GDN state:** `ss.gdn_state` is `float*`; each recurrent layer addresses its state and convolution tail as float in prefill. This is the source candidate most directly matched by DAMP. A valid FP16-storage experiment would need FP32 read/compute and a clearly specified cast/roundtrip point; changing arithmetic as well would confound the storage question.
- **QSA cache:** QSA K/V persistent cache has separate `core::QsaState` formats and mode controls, including quantized variants. Hold KV format and cache mode fixed in a recurrent-state experiment. KV quantization is not evidence about GDN state precision.
- **Other temporary / accumulation paths:** `Dm`, router scores, PLE/residual mixed rows and relevant attention scratch are float buffers in this path. Do not infer they can be halved from the GDN study. The output logits path is also a distinct quality-sensitive operation.

The only source-supported candidate identified here is a new GDN persistent-state FP16 storage arm, retaining FP32 update and output arithmetic where feasible. The existing `Hh` is already low precision and needs a dedicated counterfactual if its effect is in question. No source modification is proposed in this report.

## Minimum actual comparison and 262144 sensitivity contract

Use the identical checkpoint/weight files, tokenizer, prompt and fixed target IDs, model geometry, routing, quantized-weight backend, CPU/GPU expert split, KV format, PLE mode, sampler and binary/runtime. Compare fresh independent sessions for (A) current FP32 GDN state and (B) FP16 GDN state with FP32 state reconstruction/update/readout. Record exactly when casts occur, rounding/subnormal/overflow handling, and whether prefill chunks write an FP16 state between chunks. Run repeated A/A controls to estimate evaluator and kernel variation. Do not compare unlike devices/configurations as the only arms.

At 262,144 input tokens, use the production full-context path and multiple held-out, content-diverse token-ID documents with retrieval keys near early, middle, late, and final positions. Include long code/instruction and factual-recall tasks. Exercise prompt lengths around actual chunk seams and a full 262,144-token prompt, then score fixed teacher-forced target rows at absolute positions across the prompt, including near the end; test long decode from the final state too. The source/evaluator must demonstrate all 262,144 prompt cells were consumed, not just that an allocation/context setting accepted the length. A 128K or repeated-token-extended 64K benchmark is not a substitute.

Retain complete FP32 vocabulary logits for matched fixed targets, validate exact row count and finiteness, and report full-distribution KL (both directions or JS), target-token negative log likelihood/perplexity deltas, max/mean logit error and top-1/margin flips, along with task accuracy/pass rate and exact benchmark settings. Use enough examples/repeats to include reasoning and code tasks; report counts and uncertainty, not just a single aggregate. Record first-divergent layers/state diagnostics when already available. If testing `Hh`, create a third independent FP32-Hh arm with all other dtypes and grouping held fixed; compare it directly to the existing FP16-Hh fallback. Do not attribute combined Hh/state/accumulation changes to one dtype.

For an RTX B570 transfer decision, rerun the same exact checkpoint, test corpus, and paired precision arms on that device. The Qwen3.6 study’s software and hardware do not make its small measured differences a B570 result. Verify the selected math kernel, storage casts, determinism/noise and full-context allocation on B570; then report device/compiler/runtime/model hash alongside quality and performance. Performance wins are a separate measured question.
