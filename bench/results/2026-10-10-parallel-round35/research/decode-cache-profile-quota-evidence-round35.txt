# Round 35 — Does the active profile justify rebalancing 128 expert slots?

## Verdict

The arithmetic in round34 is correct for the frozen H command and model: 128 slots over 48 MoE layers yield a quotient of two, with the entire 32-slot remainder assigned to layer 47. I independently checked the actual profile file and mapped its ranked pairs through the current admission rule. The profile does let us describe exactly which 128 pairs are admitted and what a rank-based redistribution would select. It does **not**, by itself, justify changing quotas: the file stores an ordering, not route counts, probability, trace provenance, or validation that the order predicts this workload. The closed H histogram is aggregate and has no layer/expert axis. Keep the current quota until exact routed-expert evidence shows repeated cache misses are concentrated in other layers and that the rank-based candidate improves real cache hits. No implementation, execution, or speed claim is recommended from the current evidence.

## Frozen model, command, and source behavior

The committed root review `bench/results/2026-10-10-native-wrapper-matrix/REPORT.md` says the actual pack is 48 MoE layers and 512 experts per layer. The closed histogram receipt's recorded `argv` confirms `--expert-cache 128 --expert-profile data/expert-profile.bin --expert-cache-per-layer --adapt-swaps 0`, with `--max-context 262144`, `--kv-resident 32768`, `--prefill 8192`, MTP/spec4 and split verification. Startup records `expert_slots=128`, 325 MiB, and context 262144. The profile's relative path is the H worktree's `data/expert-profile.bin`; its SHA-256 is `8f59b4aa8873209dff11c11e37bcda9529a1335b724a1afeea37bf6388975baf`.

In frozen H source, `ExpertCache::layer_slot_range` computes `q=slots/n_layers`, then gives layer `l` `[l*q,(l+1)*q)` except the final layer, whose end is all slots (`sycl/src/core/expert_cache.cpp:759-767`). For `128/48`, layers 0–46 each own 2 and layer 47 owns 34. `admit` refuses a full layer's next expert and does not borrow another layer's unused range or evict an existing expert (`:773-787`). Startup profile fill walks the ranked pair list once and attempts every pair in per-layer mode; it does not stop the whole pass when one layer fills (`sycl/src/program/generate.cpp:4548-4571`). Runtime adaptation is off in this command (`--adapt-swaps 0`). Therefore this is a fixed profile-preloaded set throughout decode, not a cache that grows by phase or reallocates unused slots.

The command and slot split are based on the repository's `REPORT.md` and immutable request record, not inferred from a hit counter. A caveat is that the record stores a relative profile path and no `cwd` field; the hash above is the matching frozen H worktree file inspected for this assignment.

## Read-only profile parse and admission simulation

The local reader specifies a little-endian `STRP` header of five 32-bit integers after the magic, followed by `n_ranked` `(uint16 layer, uint16 expert)` pairs (`sycl/src/core/expert_cache.cpp:142-219`). The writer then appends a 32-bit rank-to-slot table (`:229-265`). I parsed only the `n_ranked` pair records the reader consumes, then independently checked the table-length tail against the writer's format. The file is 196,632 bytes: 24 header bytes + 98,304 ranked-pair bytes + 98,304 table bytes. Header is `(version=1, n_layers=48, n_expert=512, slots=24576, n_ranked=24576)`. All 24,576 consumed pairs are in range and unique, covering exactly 512 pairs in every layer. The extra 98,304 bytes are the rank table, not a second ranked list or per-pair heat data.

Simulating `ExpertCache::admit` over that ranked list with the frozen 128-slot quotas accepts exactly 128 pairs and refuses the remaining 24,448 after their layer quotas fill. Each of layers 0–46 gets its first two entries in profile order; layer 47 gets its first 34. Its 34 admitted profile positions are: 2, 60, 104, 122, 183, 245, 325, 486, 675, 684, 772, 783, 789, 858, 887, 982, 993, 1012, 1013, 1032, 1073, 1082, 1090, 1111, 1112, 1132, 1188, 1189, 1219, 1220, 1260, 1475, 1486, and 1584 (zero-based). Thus the long final-layer quota is real, and the file contains candidates for filling slots elsewhere if the ordering has useful meaning.

For comparison only, I also simulated round34's proposed alternative: keep 2 slots per layer, then award the 32 surplus slots to the globally earliest profile-ranked additional pairs. That selects surplus entries at global zero-based ranks 26–92, distributed across 16 other layers; the resulting quotas range from 2 to 6, and layer47 returns to 2. The selected set's maximum global rank falls from 1,584 to 1,283, and its mean rank from 323.7 to 111.2. These are properties of a deterministic ranking simulation, **not** observed routing frequencies, cache hits, decode work, or performance.

The profile format itself has no frequency/count column. `tools/make_profile.py` documents that it keeps a base profile's ordering first and that the shipped base already ranks all 24,576 pairs; so an ordinary trace append with that base cannot alter its ranking. Its `--reorder` mode is required for traces to influence the top of that fully populated list. This makes profile provenance material. Although `generate.cpp` labels the loaded profile "ranked by routing frequency" in a startup message, the checked file cannot verify what generated its order or how predictive that order is for the present two prompts and 32K decode workload. The repo tool documentation also describes the profile as an ordering, not a stored frequency table. Do not treat the numeric rank as a count or probability.

## What the closed histogram cannot establish

The root's H report records four passing fresh 32K requests and aggregate caller jobs, routed-entry totals, and cache hits. The report explicitly says those counters are not every routed expert, row-task fragments, DRAM traffic, or worker-tail time. Because they are not keyed by layer or expert, they cannot show (a) which layer generated each hit/miss, (b) whether the final layer's 34 slots are used or mostly idle, (c) which repeatedly requested nonresident experts would be admitted by a different quota, or (d) whether a candidate's additional resident pairs would replace hits elsewhere. A full 24,576-pair static ranking does not fill those gaps.

Minimum useful exact diagnostic: at the actual expert-cache lookup/classification point, emit per request and per decode phase a compact `(layer, expert, route/job count, hit count, miss/refused count)` summary, plus the cache's resident set/slot quota at the start and end. Keep job counts distinct from routed-token entries (`nt`) as in the current H report. This exact pair histogram can be joined offline with the profile and simulated 128-slot candidate sets to compute current versus candidate hit opportunities on the same routes. At minimum, layer totals could reveal gross concentration but would not establish whether a particular next-ranked expert is recurrent; pair-keyed counts are required to evaluate the proposed static selection. Record prefill separately and do not add it to decode counts.

## Prior work reconciliation

Related local reports already cover the neighboring cache ideas. `moe-decode-cpu.txt` proposed testing equal per-layer 2→3 slots, with actual per-layer byte accounting and both 32K restored and 256K decode gates; this is an unrun capacity increase and costs additional VRAM. Round35 is not a repeat of that test: it evaluates whether the existing 128-slot budget is skewed by the quotient remainder. `layer-expert-cache-footprint-contract-round10.txt` analyzes `open_sized` for the temporary one-layer prefill cache and notes that explicit counts preserve their current semantics; it does not justify changing the persistent decode quota. `decode-retained-vram-after-first-prefill-round10.txt` finds the existing main-cache release is prompt-only and restored before decode; it cannot increase steady-state decode slots. `prefill-dequant-cache-layer-lifetime-round9.txt` concerns transient prefill dequant scratch, not the decode expert cache. Round34 proposed rank-based redistribution conditional on layer-resolved evidence; this report tightens that condition because rank provenance and actual layer hit counts remain unknown. The report registry v31 also preserves the other current boundaries: no blanket cache resizing, no claim from geometry alone, full 256K C rejection remains unresolved, and successful H histogram diagnostics are not performance eligible.

## External implementation context (not B570 evidence)

An implementer write-up for a llama.cpp fork describes a trace-profile-driven static cache with `--moe-cache-slots` configured per layer, merged contrasting code/chat router traces, and explicit slot-count/headroom tuning. It reports results on its own RTX 3060/other configurations and warns to retain VRAM for KV/runtime allocations. This supports the general practice of using workload routing traces and slot budgets; it does not support Strata's 128/48 remainder choice or predict this B570/Ryzen result. [Shadowgraph llama.cpp implementation and tuning notes](https://shadowgraph.io/thecodacus/llama.cpp).

The FineMoE EuroSys paper likewise uses searched expert probabilities together with observed cache visit frequency in expert-cache priority/eviction, and reports prefill and decode separately. Its adaptive frequency-aware policy is distinct from this static fixed-quota cache; its six-RTX-3090 testbed and results do not transfer to the target PC. The relevant lesson is to measure/cache-key the routed experts and report decode separately, not to infer value from a profile rank. [FineMoE paper, §§4.5–6](https://intellisys.haow.us/assets/pdf/Hanfei_FineMoE_EuroSys26.pdf).

## Action and preserved gates

No quota change is justified now. The actionable next step is to collect the compact exact per-`(layer, expert)` lookup counts on the same cache policy, then replay them offline through the existing 128-slot selection and the candidate rank redistribution. If that replay shows the candidate lowers cache hits or current misses are not concentrated where it adds capacity, stop. If it predicts higher hit coverage, only then consider a controlled fresh comparison; it must retain the exact model, `--max-context 262144`, `--kv-resident 32768`, and 8192-token prefill, and keep prompt processing, cold decode after a fresh prompt, and restored-state decode as separate results. Preserve the existing full head, all-66-state, generated IDs/logprobs, MTP, health/fault, normal exit/cleanup, and complete physical-262144 lifecycle gates. A 32K histogram pass or offline replay alone cannot clear the unresolved full-context rejection. No speed forecast is made.
