# External evidence for service-weighted MoE offload objectives — round 71

## Scope and decision

Read-only external source review, retrieved 2026-10-10, for CPU/RAM plus limited-VRAM MoE execution on the frozen Strata path (Ryzen 5 5600X, Intel Arc B570 10 GiB; source worktree `diag-sycl-cache-route-pairs-v0141-20261010` at `66aaec3a66ad9ed12e6d042cffe743c47e364432`). No repository source, tests, build, model/server, GPU, profiler, Git state, or cleanup was touched. This report adds external implementation/paper evidence about objective choice and phase dependence; it does not propose adopting an external runtime or change any measured status.

The defensible objective is phase-specific avoided *measured service time*, with resource and quality constraints, rather than routed-expert counts or cache hit rate alone. Prefill and decode have different route aggregation, CPU batch efficiency, upload amortization, and latency constraints. Track: (a) resident-expert CPU service saved, (b) source read/assembly cost where weights are loaded, (c) host-to-device transfer/admission cost, (d) synchronization/queue delay, and (e) any cache churn or lost residency. Keep prompt-prefill results separate from first-token-to-decode restoration and steady decode. The external literature supports this framing but supplies no calibrated values for Strata's packed IQ/Q2 formats, worker pool, 5600X, Arc B570, or service distribution.

## Primary sources and what transfers

**Fiddler (ICLR 2025) makes the CPU-compute-versus-copy crossover explicit.** Its latency model compares CPU execution against CPU-to-GPU weight transfer plus GPU execution, and selects per-layer plans based on batch/input shape. It finds CPU execution can win for small batch because transferring expert weights costs more, while larger input/batch can make CPU compute too slow and favour moving weights to the GPU. This argues against treating one route frequency as a universal placement score: the value of caching/offloading depends on token multiplicity and the phase's shape. The paper's Mixtral/24-GB-GPU results and model are not measurements for Strata. Sources: [ICLR paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/8cd1ce03ea58b3d7dfd809e4d42f08ea-Paper-Conference.pdf), [authors' implementation](https://github.com/efeslab/fiddler).

**KTransformers demonstrates a deliberate phase-specific hybrid plan, on different CPUs and quantization.** The SOSP'25 paper and its technical documentation separate CPU expert backends, GPU expert count/placement, long-context layerwise prefill, and AMX execution. This is a useful implementation precedent for treating prefill as its own scheduling regime, rather than carrying decode cache rankings into a long prompt unchanged. Its AMX-optimized kernels, supported quantized layouts, and evaluated systems do not transfer as performance evidence to Zen 3 AVX2 or Strata's exact GGUF expert blobs. Sources: [SOSP'25 paper](https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/publication/ktransformers-unleashing-the-full-potential-of-cpu/gpu-hybrid-inference-for-moe-models/SOSP25-chen.pdf), [heterogeneous inference docs](https://ktransformers.net/en/docs/technical-work/heterogeneous-inference), [SGLang integration issue](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/11425).

**SGLang's CPU roadmap distinguishes prefilling optimization from hybrid MoE scheduling.** The maintainers describe CPU thread/cache blocking to avoid duplicate weight reads for large input M as a prefill-specific target, separately list sequential and pipelined GPU-CPU MoE offload, and identify long-context prefill/decode and NUMA-aware expert balancing as innovation areas. The roadmap's 50–80% prefill figure is a target/estimate associated with that CPU blocking work, not a result on Strata or a claim for this machine. The key transfer is that large-M CPU service has its own reuse and memory-traffic behavior; don't infer it from NT=1/2 decode service. Source: [SGLang CPU roadmap issue #8281](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/8281).

**A vLLM RFC reports a large prefill/decode route-shape difference, but remains a proposal.** Its incremental expert cache RFC states one GPT-OSS example where batched prefill loads O(number of experts) after deduplicating selected IDs, versus O(sequence length × top-k) naive sequential loads; it also reports different numbers of distinct experts for prefill and decode in the motivating trace. These details support measuring unique expert sets and load counts separately by phase. Its production numbers are an independent tinyserve implementation on RTX PRO 2000 / GPT-OSS-20B, not vLLM-merged validation or evidence for this project; the RFC's stated cache design and numbers must be treated as proposal/author report. Source: [vLLM RFC #38256](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/38256).

**PowerInfer's hot/cold policy is based on neuron activation locality, not CPU expert service.** It preloads hot activated neurons on GPU and computes cold neurons on CPU, using adaptive prediction and sparse operators. This establishes a consumer-PC precedent for locality-aware partitioning, but the unit (neurons within dense-model layers), workload/prediction, and GPU path differ from whole routed expert blobs. Do not transfer its speedups or treat neuron popularity as a Strata expert-cost oracle. Source: [SOSP'24 paper](https://ipads.se.sjtu.edu.cn/_media/publications/song-sosp24.pdf), [project](https://github.com/stephlow/powerinfer).

**Intel llm-scaler documents a platform stack, not a suitable target-side expert-cost result.** Its public repository is a vLLM/XPU distribution. The reviewed material did not reveal a comparable validated service-cost optimizer for a Ryzen AVX2 CPU plus Arc B570/Strata GGUF layout. Keep Intel/XPU throughput claims and AMX guidance separate from this host's CPU ISA and engine. Source: [intel/llm-scaler](https://github.com/intel/llm-scaler).

## Objective implications for Strata

1. **Decode residency / replacement:** if later justified by route-resolved evidence, compare policies under a fixed byte/slot budget using held-out per-layer routes and measured per-format, per-role CPU service. A candidate value should approximate expected CPU service avoided by keeping an expert resident, then subtract measured admission/copy and churn cost. A route count or hit alone is not milliseconds. R50/R51 already reviewed global compatible-group LRU and a formula-based cost gate in `moe-autopilot`; these are policy analogues, not a new runtime recommendation. Do not duplicate that candidate here.
2. **Prefill:** aggregate the actual prompt's selected IDs by layer and unique expert, retain token multiplicity, and measure the production path's M-dependent CPU batch service and any repeated source/weight reads. Compare against the actual GPU execution plus transfer/admission cost. A cache that is useful in single-token decode can churn on prefill; conversely prompt deduplication can make loading each unique expert once preferable to per-token operations. Measure with actual request shapes and exact prompt IDs.
3. **Phase boundary:** prompt work may borrow cache-tail slots then restore/re-upload experts before decode. The saved decode service is realized only after the restored map is correct and the service cost recurs. Count/measure borrowing, assembly/upload, waits, restoration and first decode separately; don't credit prefill-route hits as decode residency benefit. R59's live-engine contract identifies that borrowing/restoration and graph/map ownership constraint.
4. **Serving objective:** report TTFT/prefill and inter-token decode separately, or an explicitly declared weighted request objective including prompt/decode lengths and service-level constraints. Avoid averaging phases into a single “cache win” without a request mix. A throughput policy can be inferior under a latency/SLA objective.

The transferable evidence is the *shape-dependent comparison*, not a policy, threshold, or expected speedup. No external source gives the exact per-role service cost, 5600X AVX2 execution time, Arc B570 transfer cost under the live engine, or end-to-end effect of a policy change. The next discriminator remains the root-owned real-weight calibration and exact prompt/route measurement; no kernel adoption or runtime cache change is supported by this review.
