## Scope and decision

Read-only review of the committed compile-OFF quiet 32K/chunk-2048 experiment, its immutable source snapshot, and the later host/event diagnostic; official Linux Xe, DRM, SYCL, and Intel VTune documentation/source review for a possible low-load environment observation. No implementation, Git operation, build, test, compiler/disassembly, profiler, inference, GPU/sysfs query, metric acquisition, environment/clock change, or cleanup was performed.

The quiet result closes the specific question whether profiling timestamp queries are required to produce the order effect: they are not. It does not identify the cause of that effect. The current evidence does not justify prioritizing the R112 event-query-deferral experiment as a cause-finding step; that item can remain as an instrumentation-specific check, but is not a primary explanation test. A read-only Xe client activity snapshot is the least intrusive plausible future observation, but it cannot separate the two arms at useful resolution without adding reads/gaps to the measured sequence. With no confirmed local field availability or access, the expected value is too low to prioritize another run. No additional environment or clock investigation is recommended now. This environment question is distinct from the existing API-observation-free gate/admission diagnostic candidate. Neither measurement implies causality or performance adoption.

## Candidate observations and contract

**Xe GT frequency sysfs (`<device>/tile#/gt#/freq0/act_freq`, `cur_freq`).** Kernel documentation defines these read-only: `act_freq` is the actual resolved frequency selected by PCODE; `cur_freq` is GuC's current request. PCODE can alter the actual running frequency according to thermal and other conditions. They describe a GT frequency, not per-process frequency, and are instantaneous observations, not a per-arm average or residency counter. The documentation does not state units; driver implementation uses Xe frequency scaling (traditionally MHz), but a collector should record the exact kernel version and treat units as unverified unless that version's source confirms them. The read paths take a runtime-PM guard; querying may affect power state, and `act_freq` can be zero while GT is in RC6 according to the GuC PC implementation. Repeated reads around short arms risk waking the device and perturbing the idle state they intend to observe. Sysfs permissions and B570 kernel/driver exposure are unknown. Do not write `min_freq`/`max_freq`.

**Xe GT idle residency (`<device>/tile#/gt#/gtidle/idle_residency_ms`).** Kernel source labels this a read-only millisecond residency counter for the GT idle state (C6/MC6 depending on GT). It is a whole-GT cumulative counter, not per client, with ms granularity and counter-wrap handling; reads also go through runtime-PM handling. Differences across a long controlled window could characterize idle residency, but 32K paired arms are short, and boundaries include arm transitions and host scheduling gaps. It cannot discriminate exact per-arm duration reliably. Exposure and permission on this B570 are unknown.

**DRM client `fdinfo` usage fields — lowest-load candidate, conditional only.** The generic DRM usage-statistics contract permits `drm-cycles-<engine>` (busy cycles) and `drm-total-cycles-<engine>` (GPU timestamp in an unspecified unit matching the busy-cycle update rate). Where implemented, utilization can be computed in the GPU clock domain. Xe documentation gives CCS fields as an implementation example. A single read-only fdinfo snapshot is host-side procfs access and avoids submitting commands or making direct hardware metric queries. For any future candidate, first establish from existing records that this exact driver exports the fields and that the target process's own fdinfo is readable under current procfs/Yama policy; both are unknown here. Associate the `drm-client-id`/device to avoid double-counting duplicated fds. Keep counter deltas in their GPU domain; the total-cycle unit has no documented conversion to seconds here. Spec allows values not to be continuously monotonic, so on a decrease retain the prior high-water value until updates catch up. These are client engine-activity/utilization indicators, not actual GT frequency or total shared-device residency.

A before/after snapshot around an entire already-planned run would have low collection frequency but only an aggregate for all 10 pairs, so it cannot explain first-vs-second arm behavior. Per-arm snapshots would require at least two procfs reads per arm and add host work and gaps between timing intervals. The read cost and its effect on this harness have not been measured. A background sampler could avoid synchronous gaps but adds an independent scheduler and sampling overhead. Therefore no such run is recommended absent new evidence that a particular order-correlated process-activity gap exists. If the root later elects to take the minimal candidate, use only an already available fdinfo path, read once immediately before and after a pre-existing whole-run window, timestamp read boundaries on the host monotonic clock, preserve raw counter keys/client identity, and interpret only GPU-domain deltas as run-aggregate activity. Do not compare fdinfo counters to SYCL timestamps.

**VTune / PTI / frequent sampling.** Intel VTune's current GPU analysis setup has explicit platform/system setup and permissions requirements, and its GPU sampling configurations are sampling instrumentation rather than passive per-arm sysfs reads. Documented sampling intervals (10–1000 microseconds for the relevant sampler configuration) are unnecessarily frequent here; the platform support of this B570/Xe2 setup and collection permissions are unknown. PTI/unitrace tracing/counter collection likewise adds instrumentation and needs capability/platform validation. Both are higher overhead and risk confounding the short order-sensitive workload. Do not prioritize either for this cause gap; no large timeline is warranted.

## Clock and interpretation boundaries

The quiet benchmark's measured number is host `steady_clock` service time around the public call plus completion wait, aggregated per arm across chunks; admission is included. SYCL `command_start`/`command_end` are device action timestamps returned as 64-bit values, and querying them blocks until the corresponding command reaches the required state. The SYCL specification does not provide a conversion tying their backend clock epoch to host monotonic time or Xe sysfs/fdinfo counters. Keep host timestamps, SYCL event timestamps, and Xe/DRM counters in separate domains; do not add or subtract them to infer wait decomposition or align sampling to kernels without an explicitly validated calibration. Read-only status does not mean zero perturbation: runtime-PM, host scheduling and sampling cadence remain confounds.
