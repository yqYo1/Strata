## EOF, QUIT, and independent process ordering

The v5 source fixes the v4 race conservatively. Before QUIT, an observed zero-read or PTY-master `EIO` is recorded (reason, raw/framer byte offsets, cached inferior exit status, last MI stop, and `quit_sent`), `protocol.finish()` validates no partial frame, and the controller raises `early transport EOF before QUIT`. Non-EIO read errors are recorded separately and raised; `BlockingIOError`/EAGAIN returns false and never sets EOF. The observation is recorded before `finish()`, so malformed partial-tail failure retains transport provenance.

After sending QUIT, the bounded loop runs up to 30 seconds. Each iteration calls controller `poll()` first, which calls the real `OwnedGdb.poll(.01)` to drain/parse GDB/MI, then checks PTY readiness. If PTY EOF is observed while MI exit is still unknown, `available_stdout()` preserves that exact unknown-at-observation record and returns without success. It does not reread after the protocol helper enters EOF. The loop continues polling until both a real helper EOF and MI-observed inferior exit/signal exist; after the loop the controller requires helper EOF, a transport record, `g.exit_code == 0`, and no signal. Thus a later normal MI exit can complete the overall boundary but cannot rewrite the earlier transport ordering. EAGAIN does not satisfy the EOF half; if EOF never arrives, the loop times out. Failure to receive an MI normal-exit record also cannot pass. `OwnedGdb` distinguishes `*stopped,reason="exited-normally"` (exit code 0), nonzero `reason="exited"`, and signalled exit in its MI parser; GDB child cleanup is separately recorded and checked.

This is a source-level ordering argument, not proof of a real ordering. The new fake fixture calls only extracted `available_stdout`, `boundary`, `encoded_failure`, and related helpers with a fake `os.read`, simple mutable GDB state, and a real bounded framer. Its EIO test demonstrates preservation when exit is unknown and confirms mutating the fake state later does not alter the captured dict. Its zero-read test starts with exit known. Its EAGAIN test leaves the fake read queue empty, causing the fake to raise `BlockingIOError`, then confirms no EOF. It does **not** execute the actual `poll()`/selector loop, `OwnedGdb.poll`, MI channel, terminal PTY master, or OS-specific EIO sequence. Therefore it does not prove that a normally exited inferior reliably emits and has its MI record parsed within the deadline, nor that EIO is delivered as modeled on the eventual run. Source and fixture both remain explicitly unexecuted evidence here.

## Bounded failure receipt and fallback

The source computes the exact JSON payload through `encoded_failure`, including its own byte count, projected aggregate count, overflow flag, and final newline, then writes those returned bytes once. If the first receipt exceeds the 1 MiB failure reserve, it serializes a reduced fallback, retains failure/math/pair/histogram/shape/protocol-boundary flags, exit status, original C/D statuses, compact cleanup flags, fault count and bounded error excerpts, and hashes/records the byte size of omitted metadata. The fallback is rejected if still over reserve. It remains unhealthy and budget-failed; an existing mathematical PASS is preserved without becoming an overall pass. An aggregate overflow is recorded as failure; helper-written bytes are not deleted or rewritten to fake compliance.

The fake fixture covers exact-reserve sizing, forced oversized metadata/fallback, ordinary math-PASS plus budget-failure preservation, sparse-file aggregate overflow, and equality between stored bytes and the declared serialized size. It checks selected final predicate and source guard strings. These are useful serialization fixtures but do not execute the full controller error lifecycle or simulate filesystem write failure. One limitation is material to diagnostics: the reduced fallback currently omits `transport_eof`, `transport_read_error`, `protocol_failure_state`, and `protocol_boundaries`. It retains only `protocol_boundary_gate_passed` plus aggregate omission hash/size. If the previous `record.json` snapshot is stale and the first failure payload exceeds reserve, the compact final record may not reveal whether transport closed by zero-read or EIO, the raw offset, or whether MI exit was known at observation. Preserve at least a compact transport reason/offset/exit-known/quit-sent tuple in the fallback (or explicitly identify its omission as part of the retained summary) before relying on the fallback as a forensic receipt. This source gap does not create a false pass, but weakens the requested failure evidence under the exact oversized-metadata case.
