## Correction to R150: full-stream copies include zero-route experts

The source walk for `stream_all` constructs `seq` from **every nonresident expert** in every layer (`prefill.cpp` around 1410). `issue_until` or the threaded issuer submits those blobs to the copy queue as the ring advances (around 1452 and 1505). The consumer later observes route IDs; a nonresident expert with zero routes returns its ring slot without dequant or product work. It does not retract a copy already submitted by the issuer. Therefore the full-stream transfer ledger counts all nonresident expert blobs, including zero-route entries. R150's statement that zero-route entries were omitted from the submitted full-stream H2D copies was incorrect; this report supersedes that statement.

For this model, the layout has 48 layers x 512 experts and total logical expert-bank extent 50,292,326,400 B. The preserved cache configuration has 3 resident slots/layer, leaving 509 nonresident expert blobs/layer: 24,432 blobs and 49,997,644,800 B per full-stream chunk. This exactly matches the 32K receipt's full-stream ledger divided over its five `full_nonresident_stream` chunks. Route-zero skips change compute/dequant counts, not the already-submitted full-stream transfer request count.

## Logical requests, physical traffic, and timing are separate

The H2D ledger records successful copy submissions with the source blob extent. It is a logical requested/copy payload and call count; it does not establish PCIe link bytes, hardware transactions, or DMA duration. The `dequant input` column sums active expert tensor payloads regardless of resident versus staged location; the decoder operates on selected active experts only. The FP16 column is full decoded output writes. Subsequent grouped FP16 GEMMs logically read those weights again. None of these byte sums is a measurement of external-memory traffic, cache traffic, or elapsed time. Physical device DRAM and host-link bytes/bandwidth were not measured in this audit.

The recorded 32K prompt times were 122,314.5 ms OFF and 122,039.1 ms ON, and their prefill TPS fields were 267.90 and 268.50 respectively. Both receipts explicitly mark `performance_eligible=false`; these values are diagnostic elapsed-time records only, are not a qualified comparison, and support no speedup claim. The 64 and 2,049 timings are likewise ineligible. The per-layer route and transfer equality across off/on does show the compact toggle did not alter the workload census in these closed runs.

## What this says about a future 64K, fixed-T8192 arm

For 65,536 prompt IDs, the ordinary native path batches 65,535 rows. At fixed T=8192 that is seven full chunks plus one 8,191-row tail. All eight chunk sizes are >=2,048. With the same `ring>8`, ExpertSource, 48x512 model layout, and 3 resident experts/layer, source selects `full_nonresident_stream` for all eight chunks; unlike the 32K/6144 tail (2,047 rows), the final 8,191-row tail does not switch to routed staging. Thus route-zero experts remain candidates for unused full-stream copies in every future chunk. If the same cache slots and extents hold, the source-requested H2D envelope is 8 x 24,432 = 195,456 blob copies, 399,981,158,400 B (49,997,644,800 B/chunk). This is a conditional source/layout extrapolation, not an observed 64K transfer receipt; compact-on T8192 admission, cache residency, physical traffic and time remain unqualified.

The 32K records quantify that route-zero copies are not negligible in source byte accounting: 16.47 GB over five 6144 full-stream chunks, plus 7.41 GB in one 2048 full-stream chunk. A zero-copy optimization is therefore worth measuring if host-copy issue or link service is exposed. At T8192 the larger batch changes route unions, so the 6144 zero-route ratio cannot be projected as a saving. Whether this matters to elapsed 64K prefill depends on actual host staging/link timing and overlap with compute; the current ledger alone cannot say. Retain route-zero copy calls as a candidate work source, not as established bottleneck.

`Gemm::native` is also invoked from the PLE batched path inside its `SB` loop when a native PLE key is selected. Its full-matrix dequant/GEMM call count therefore depends on `ceil(T/SB)` for the actual region and selected PLE tensor. These six receipts do not expose PLE `SB` or a per-call trace, so I do not assign a PLE repeated-dequant total here. A 64K/8192 receipt should record the actual PLE branch, key type/shape and number of SB calls; source's number of Prefill chunks alone is not enough to infer them.

## Bounded conclusion

At 32K, the route/transfer census is identical OFF and ON and establishes exact per-layer type cohorts, active route-bearing expert counts, resident/nonresident splits, zero-route counts, full expert bank bytes, source-level H2D copy requests, and active expert dequant payload sums for these six completed diagnostics. It corrects the full-stream behavior: all nonresident blobs are copied per full-stream chunk, while zero-route entries skip only downstream compute and dequant. The reported bytes are requests/logical payloads, not physical traffic. The future 64K/T8192 projection remains conditional, and all recorded timing remains ineligible for performance conclusions.
