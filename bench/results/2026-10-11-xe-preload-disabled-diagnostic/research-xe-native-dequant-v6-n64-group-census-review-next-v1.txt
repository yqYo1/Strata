What ON actually groups

ON's old individual-wrapper counters are zero, while its 48-row group census records, independently per axis, 6,260 normally admitted group calls covering 7,063 logical experts, zero declined calls and zero fallback experts. Thus the aggregate mean coverage per admitted call is 7,063/6,260 = 1.128 experts for GU and Down. This is a modest call-count reduction of 803 per axis (11.36% from the OFF individual count), not evidence that most batches contain multiple experts: individual windows are not logged. With API capacity 16 and no declines, the aggregate excess of 803 covered experts implies at least 54 multi-expert admissions and at most 803, but does not establish their lengths or distribution. Do not label 16 as the actual width.

ON recorded 6,260 flushes and 5,794 held-reuse flushes (92.6%), plus 6,951 released slots. Those counters describe flush/release causes at layer scope. They support the source explanation that slot reuse often closes windows on this routed STAGE8 case; they do not reveal how many experts each individual flush carried. The per-layer logical total equals GU and Down covered total for all 48 rows; no axis fell back. Census metadata explicitly labels units as host normal-return admissions/logical expert coverage, window validation as aggregate bounds only, and performance inference false. It also does not independently prove GPU completion.

What a 32K ON review still needs

A 32K ON receipt must re-establish the unchanged six-chunk routing/H2D counts and strict output/selected-logprob result while capturing the ON group census for every layer/chunk. In particular, report group admissions, declines, covered and fallback experts separately for GU and Down; check per-axis coverage plus fallback against the logical expanded-expert total; account for tail/skip flushes and held-slot reuse; and preserve the per-row bounds and capture-validity checks. Full-stream and routed phases need separate interpretation. For this v6 caller, a normal group-call return means submission only, and the aggregate observer does not report individual group sizes or independently establish device completion. A successful small N64 pair cannot fill these 32K coverage and lifetime gates.

Performance claims remain separate. The previous service-bound report's component timing is from an earlier standalone API component experiment (resident inputs, n=1/8/16), not measured time inside v6 model prefill. The fixed v2 API source modifies pointer selection within two group kernels, not the prefill caller; current v6's uncommitted prefill source pin is a distinct caller revision. Neither the older component medians nor the old 32K OFF 269.159 t/s reference can be treated as ON latency, projected saving, or evidence of a win. v6 N64 diagnostic prompt times are 4,939.9 ms OFF and 4,744.5 ms ON, but each is a single short diagnostic and both receipts disqualify performance; no performance conclusion follows.
