## What remains supported about prefill and decode

In this exact source, the batch flag is read only by the Prefill implementation; full-text search of the specified worktree's `src/` and `include/` finds that sole product-code reference. The branch operates on prefill native-expert dequant buffers and the prefill compute stream, before the caller's unchanged group GEMMs. No decode math, decode expert dispatch, token sampling, session decode kernel, cache replacement policy, or token-graph selection branch is keyed by this flag.

`src/program/generate.cpp` creates a local `Prefill` object, calls `prefill.run` before entering the token loop, optionally refills slots lent to prefill, and then begins token timing at the token-loop `t0`. `Prefill::run` drains its pipeline and synchronizes its compute stream before returning. `Prefill::release` synchronizes its owned streams and frees owned allocations at object destruction; there is no explicit `prefill.release()` or `prefill.reset()` between the prefill call and token loop. For the supplied 32K receipts, `--no-prefill-borrow` is set, so their path does not use the optional lent-slot refill. This establishes the order and applicable source-controlled transition. It does not establish a batch-specific decode-state or cache-recovery mechanism.

The distinction remains: a decode timing immediately after this changed prefill path can reflect the immediately preceding run's state and scheduling, but such carry-over is not a decode-kernel change. The 32K diagnostic pair still reports one 16-token decode sequence per arm (2599.6 ms OFF / 2258.7 ms ON), each receipt still has `performance_eligible=false`, and this is not enough to adopt or reject a decode kernel. The persistent source and caller path make the treatment more credible as a prefill change; they do not turn this single post-prefill decode observation into an independent decode performance result.

## Minimum next evidence and limits

Keep the existing Main-owned balanced quiet v2 campaign, with at least three independent paired samples (the current plan has four); collect prefill and decode outcomes separately and retain all pair-level measurements. For the decode field, the defensible label is “first 16 decode tokens immediately following this matched 32K prefill,” with matching inputs/settings, normal close and strict correctness/health/fault gates. Report the first pair's prompt and decode measurements separately from subsequent pairs; do not select an arm based on one observed decode value. Since this change is prefill-only, those samples can assess system-level carry-over into cold decode, not a decode-kernel adoption decision. A future decoder change would need its own at-least-three matched decode measurements with the decoder actually changed and its inputs, residency and timing scope controlled.

No other expert-cache records, cache recovery records or decode-specific auxiliary captures were opened or validated in this correction. No claim about their contents or behavior is made.
