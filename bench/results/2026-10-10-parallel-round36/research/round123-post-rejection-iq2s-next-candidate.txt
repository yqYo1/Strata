# R123 — post-rejection IQ2_S NT1 candidate review

Date: 2026-10-10. Read-only review of the rejected exact-output IQ2_S/Q8_K NT1 register-index candidate, its linked code, and external implementations/writeups. No source edit, build, compiler/disassembler run, test, benchmark, inference, GPU/device query, payload access, Git operation, active-run output inspection, or cleanup was performed. The only new artifact is this report.

## Decision

No new candidate is qualified. The register-index candidate is decisively rejected by its closed actual-cohort screen: all 12 cells across three processes lost to both trait and the direct-index control. The register candidate's median was 22.9–31.8% slower than trait and 19.9–23.0% slower than direct. Direct itself was about 5–6% slower than trait. The existing screen decision and captures are in `/home/yayoi/ghq/github.com/yqYo1/Strata/.worktree/perf-sycl-iq2s-actual-cohort-timing-20261010/bench/results/2026-10-10-iq2s-caller-dot-screen` (closed at `bd01b72ed35e89dd5de9b41abeeaab120ee65c9e`). The corrected candidate source snapshot manifest is `bf44394cdbe744514015b756c9560702560e1778ba62868b63732100c5be4c90`; production snapshot manifest begins `c0214a`. This rejection is supported by the closed measurements, not by external analogy.

Captured linked assembly shows why the register transformation is not an attractive base for another round: it adds a dependent scalar high-table read, byte unpacking and eight `vpextrw` index extractions before the same eight scalar grid-table reads per two groups. The direct control forms indices with shifts/masks/ORs and makes those same grid reads. The register variant's smaller local area (0x20 vs 0x28) did not offset its added dependency chain in any tested cell. No counters were captured, so this is a code-shape explanation, not a proven causal attribution.

## R116 sign-scratch hypothesis

R116's sign-scratch idea remains a plausible, narrow codegen hypothesis: the candidate and direct helper copy the 32-byte sign vector to stack and issue repeated memory `vpbroadcastd` loads from that scratch; trait broadcasts directly from source bytes. Reading four-byte words directly from `qs + 32 + offset` via `memcpy` might remove that stack roundtrip while preserving sign values and arithmetic order. But the scratch sequence is shared by candidate and direct, while the rejected candidate is 20–32% slower than direct. The reports contain no counters or isolated sign-only timing that attributes a material part of that loss to the scratch sequence. Thus the sign idea does not qualify as the next candidate and should be deprioritized; it is not disproven as a small optimization.

If revisited, require an immutable one-factor variant and an exact linked-code gate first: no 32-byte scratch store/reloads, no new spills, unchanged sign mapping, unchanged ordered FMA and reduction. Only then run bitwise parity and a fresh paired screen against trait and direct on the established cells. Do not combine it with index changes. A source edit without that emitted-code proof is not justified.

## External implementation and writeup survey

- [llama.cpp discussion #5152](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/5152) is a first-person AVX2 sign-expansion discussion for IQ2_XS. Its proposed vectorization changes a different format's sign representation/decoding and was reported on an older Intel CPU. It is not a format-preserving IQ2_S port, nor Zen 3 evidence.
- [llama.cpp issue #26450](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/26450) reports IQ2_S AVX2 gather results on an Intel i9-13900K: the cited P-core result rises from 5.78 to 7.15 GB/s, while E cores are slower. This is Intel-specific, not Zen 3, and gather is already among the rejected directions; it supplies no new candidate.
- [ik_llama.cpp discussion #8](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/8) describes faster IQK paths through different quant formats and reusable dequantization/matrix-multiply work. Those are storage-format and multirow changes, not exact-format IQ2_S/Q8_K NT1 replacements.
- The first-person [FrankenMoE-CUDA experiment log](https://github.com/Endorpheen/FrankenMoE-CUDA/blob/main/experiments/EXPERIMENTS.md) reports that its plain IQ2_S dot code matches upstream/ik_llama and has no faster plain kernel ready to port; its multirow fused dequant experiments win at Ny≥8 but lose at Ny=1. Its CPU model is not pinned to Zen 3, and its timings are not transferable to this target. This is a useful caution against assuming a multirow win transfers to NT1, not performance evidence for Strata.
- The [AMD Family 19h optimization guide](https://www.lsferreira.net/public/knowledge-base/x86/upos/amd_zen3.pdf) and [Agner Fog instruction tables](https://www.agner.org/optimize/instruction_tables.pdf) provide architecture/instruction context, including Zen 3 FMA scheduling facts, but no IQ2_S kernel experiment. Hiding an ordered FMA dependency by splitting accumulators would change arithmetic grouping and violate exact-output requirements. Scheduling independent decode ahead while keeping the exact FMA sequence is only speculative; no linked-code evidence suggests a gain, and extra live vectors risk register pressure.

Across this survey, no external source provides a pinned, format-preserving IQ2_S/Q8_K AVX2 NT1 implementation or Zen 3 result that escapes the already-screened gather/index/sign directions. No alternate candidate is nominated.
