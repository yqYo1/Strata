## Measurements and interpretation

| current stock, MMQ on | 118.5, 118.4, 118.9 | 118.5 | 4.2, 2.8, 3.4 | 3.4 |
| current stock, MMQ off | 182.2, 175.3, 170.1 | 175.3 | 2.8, 3.7, 2.5 | 2.8 |

For this matched stock-client shape, old PP is about 2.90x the current MMQ-on median and 1.96x the current MMQ-off median. That establishes a substantial end-to-end prefill performance gap between these measured builds/configurations. It does not isolate the cause: source, compiler/runtime, and active prefill route differ, and the old compact/layout controls have no matched current arm in these records. The current MMQ-on and MMQ-off arms also show that the prefill route matters materially inside the current source (175.3 vs 118.5 median), but the comparison does not identify whether MMQ alone, or its associated settings and numeric effects, explains that delta.

Decode rates are much closer and less stable. Old median 4.1 is numerically above current MMQ-on 3.4 and MMQ-off 2.8, but all ranges overlap: 2.8–7.3, 2.8–4.2, and 2.5–3.7. Each arm has only three timed requests with 24 generated tokens, so the decode portion is short. The old samples fall 7.3 → 4.1 → 2.8; current MMQ-on fluctuates 4.2, 2.8, 3.4; acceptance and offered counts vary. Old offered/accepted drafts were 30/13, 42/9, 45/8; MMQ-on 30/15, 45/10, 33/12; MMQ-off 45/10 in each recorded sample. These actual stock draft counters show materially different verify work. Do not use R497 lookup-row counts as proposal counts. Cache state, warmup/order and proposal acceptance remain separate decode variables; the short sequence cannot tell which explains the samples. The evidence supports no reliable decode-regression magnitude or decode-component attribution.

The old 449.7/16.6 result cannot be used to claim a decode win: its 64-token output, split-on scheduling, larger chunk and repeated-process/later-read aggregation differ. R490's paired old control/integrated decode ranges overlap; that historical work supports its prefill improvement, not a decode improvement. RESTORE 22.165 is likewise a distinct phase and not an interchangeable stock decode number.

## Component context and next minimum comparison

R500 ranks verifier split/grouping, old Q8_K AVX2 activation quantization, and current IQ NT1 dispatch as testable decode candidates, but neither stock4096 record has split enabled. R505 narrows `STRATA_IQ_SINGLE_EXACT` to eligible NT1 IQ3_XXS/IQ3_S/IQ2_S gate/up CPU misses, and requires process-start control and actual route counts. Neither report supplies end-to-end causal evidence. R501 identifies old compact=2 and QSA attention layout=1 as plausible prefill recovery candidates; source inspection alone does not attribute the gap. R502–R504 concern copy-queue/event and threaded skip-generation contracts, not proof that those mechanisms caused these stock numbers. Keep old/current source, toolchain, compact, attention-layout, MMQ, queue, and cache differences distinct; do not select one as the cause from this comparison.

The smallest useful next comparison is a Main-controlled same-current-build A/B that keeps the exact stock client/input, context, chunk 4,096, MMQ=0 FP16 fallback, output length, cache/KV geometry, queue settings and request order fixed, and toggles only compact workspace off versus `STRATA_PREFILL_COMPACT=2`. Retain prefill and decode phases separately, and record exact output IDs plus per-request draft offers/accepts; do not infer decode benefit from a prefill change. If that isolates a prefill recovery, test attention layout 1 as a separate one-factor arm at the same admitted allocation. A current-source 8,192 chunk is a separate capacity/tuning factor, not part of that first comparison. For decode, first use a longer fixed-output stock workload with repeated matched requests and stable cache/warmup policy; then test `--spec-split` alone, recording actual group/T counts, CPU-miss jobs, and accepted/offered drafts. Test IQ NT1 and activation-quantizer changes separately with eligible-route counts and fixed activation/numerical parity. Main controls any further run; this report makes no change or adoption recommendation.
