# R210 — Measured envelopes versus conditional model demand

Date: 2026-10-10 (Asia/Tokyo). Read-only quantitative comparison using the closed round53 archive, R206–R209, root corrections and primary hardware/STREAM documentation. The archive’s five hardware stages all passed; the baseline and pool0 model diagnostics each passed output/math gates for three requests. The fresh requests processed exactly 32,768 actual batched input positions. The pool0 setting is the existing automatic mode, so this is not a changed-scheduler treatment or clean throughput comparison. No test, build, device/process inspection, GPU/model/profiler/PMU run, Git operation, service action or cleanup was performed. Only this report was written.

## Decision

The measured envelopes make 1,000 prefill tokens/s numerically plausible only under strong scheduling assumptions; they do not show that the current model route can attain it. If the historical approximately 190 GB of queued expert-copy payload applied unchanged to a 32,768-position request and used the measured large pinned-host-USM H2D rate, transfer alone would take about 29.46 seconds of the 32.77-second target budget. That leaves about 3.30 seconds for all other work if transfer is serialized. The expert GEMM work estimate is about 154.62 TFLOP; measured rotate8 M=80/160 GEMM rates imply about 6.30–11.46 seconds of GEMM service under a shape-pure extrapolation. If copy and compute serialize, their combined estimate exceeds the target; if they overlap almost completely, transfer dominates and leaves only about 3.30 seconds for the remaining path. Neither serial nor perfect overlap is established by the receipts.

For 70 decode tokens/s, the 64-output target is 0.914 seconds. The matched diagnostic has 25–27 verification windows and a physical 64-output decode time of 4.37–4.82 seconds, while its host pool phase spans total about 3.64–3.74 seconds per request. This prioritizes phase and overlap attribution but is diagnostic-only evidence. At the same MTP grouping, the logged 3.03–3.18 GB/window is about 86.9–89.4 GB/s of *logical submitted expert extents* at 70 tokens/s. It is not a DRAM rate. The measured six-core stream cases do not establish an absolute RAM ceiling, and no direct conversion from those logical extents to seconds is justified.

## Conditional prefill budget at 1,000 tokens/s

A 32,768-actual-position prompt at 1,000 input tokens/s has a total wall budget of 32.768 seconds. R207’s older closed 32K copy receipt reports about 190 GB of *queued logical expert bytes*. This is a scenario input only: it belongs to a prior 32,767-batched-position route and is not a measured byte count for the current 32,768-position diagnostic. If exactly 190 decimal GB were transferred over the current probe’s pinned host-USM path at its median 6.448533 GB/s, the inclusive transfer span would be `190 / 6.448533 = 29.46 s`, or 89.9% of the target budget. At the lowest of seven samples the estimate is 29.48 s. It leaves 3.30 s at the median for all remaining exposed work if serial. The pageable-H2D median would imply 41.39 s, exceeding the budget by 8.62 s, but Strata’s actual host allocation/copy route has not been established to equal either probe arm. The external Gen4 x4 encoded data ceiling (7.8769 GB/s) would imply a 24.12 s ideal lower floor for 190 GB; it removes line coding only and is not application payload throughput.

The conditional expert GEMM work follows the source geometry `N=2560`, `K=640`, top-10 experts and 48 layers. Per expert-token row, fused GU is `2 projections × 2 FLOP/MAC × 640 × 2560 = 6,553,600 FLOP`; Down is `2 × 2560 × 640 = 3,276,800 FLOP`. If all 32,768 positions route to ten experts in all 48 layers and all such rows reach the measured GEMMs, this sums to 103.079 TFLOP GU plus 51.540 TFLOP Down, or 154.619 TFLOP total. That is 4.718592 GFLOP per input position and 4.718592 TFLOP/s of required arithmetic service at 1,000 positions/s. It excludes attention/QSA, routing, quantization/dequantization, transfer setup, synchronization and any other work; it is therefore a partial demand estimate, not total model demand.

The measured synthetic oneMKL rotate8 medians at the observed small route shapes are:

| GEMM M | GU rate | GU inclusive amortized time/call | Down rate | Down inclusive amortized time/call | Implied total service if all calls had this M |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 80 | 13.160516 TFLOP/s | 39.838 µs | 14.207360 TFLOP/s | 18.451 µs | 11.46 s |
| 160 | 25.652814 TFLOP/s | 40.876 µs | 22.547582 TFLOP/s | 23.253 µs | 6.30 s |

The inclusive amortized call times are the benchmark’s host submission-to-completion batch span divided by calls, not per-kernel device-event durations. The service estimate divides the separately summed FLOPs by each shape’s measured median rate; the matching repeated-call calculation is conditional on all routed rows being grouped into only that M. Actual call counts and M histogram weight the service time: M=80 requires twice as many calls as M=160 for the same routed rows. The seven-sample rates use synthetic values (`+/-0.125` weights and all-ones activations) and do not prove production-data cache behavior. They are helpful calibrated shape points, not a production route roof.

Under the restrictive assumption all prefill expert calls fall at M=80 or M=160 and use the rotate8 rates, the GEMM work would consume about 11.46 or 6.30 seconds respectively. Fully serial addition to the conditional 29.46-second transfer span gives 40.92 or 35.76 seconds, both over the 32.77-second target before non-GEMM work. With ideal complete overlap, the larger span is 29.46 seconds, leaving 3.30 seconds for all non-overlapped remainder. Real execution need not lie between these toy estimates: shape distribution, copy reuse, transfer/GEMM dependencies, staging, queue waits, dequantization and host scheduling are unmeasured. Thus current evidence does not prove either that 1,000 tokens/s is impossible or that it is feasible.

## Conditional 70-token/s decode budget

For 64 visible tokens, 70 tokens/s permits 0.9143 seconds total, or 14.286 ms/output. Using the same observed MTP groupings, this is at most 33.862 ms per 27-window fresh-first request or 36.571 ms per 25-window restored request; those are request-wall budgets divided by windows, not independent phase allowances.

The current pool0 fresh-first diagnostic used 27 windows, 2.370 visible tokens/window, 4,816.4 ms for 64 outputs and 3,028,757,807.41 logical expert-blob bytes/window. At the fixed target and same grouping, that is 29.531 windows/s and 89.443 GB/s of logical extents. The two restored diagnostics used 25 windows, 2.560 outputs/window, 4,385.5 and 4,374.5 ms, and 3,177,867,264 logical bytes/window: 27.344 windows/s and 86.895 GB/s logical extents at 70 tokens/s. Neither byte rate is measured DRAM traffic. Comparing 89.4 GB/s directly with the six-core 37.6 GB/s stream probe yields only a conditional thought experiment: if every submitted extent were a one-time DRAM read, it would exceed that achieved stream rate; cache reuse, actual physical line traffic and a non-absolute stream envelope are unknown. The CPU probe’s dependent read is not an absolute RAM ceiling.

Pool0 fresh GU + FF quant + Down spans were 98.911 + 0.332 + 39.147 = 138.390 ms/window; restored sums were 145.015 and 145.787 ms/window. At target output rate those recorded host spans exceed the per-window wall budgets by roughly 4.1× and 4.0×. However, the same diagnostic’s CPU-dispatch spans were 138.41 ms fresh and 145.04/145.81 ms restored; the close match is not an exclusive CPU-busy decomposition, and GPU work can overlap. The pair is explicitly excluded from clean throughput, has no per-worker busy/tail histogram or physical DRAM count, and changes no task ordering. It does show the target gap is large: 4.816 s diagnostic fresh-first (about 13.29 tok/s) versus 0.914 s target, requiring about 5.27× lower physical decode wall if that diagnostic were representative. It is not a clean performance baseline.

## Reordering versus architecture; next evidence

Reordering independent expert or row work within a layer/phase may improve locality, load balance, or overlap. If it preserves the same routes, it does not remove the top-10 arithmetic or the 190 GB scenario’s total payload by itself. Reuse-aware cache residency or transfer deduplication could change repeated transfer bytes if the implementation has an eligible reuse opportunity; that is a resource-lifetime/cache architecture change, not mere order. The decode GU → FF activation quantization → Down dependency and layer-to-layer dependency remain. A schedule change cannot safely overlap those dependent phases without explicit completion and scratch/output lifetime edges. Existing pool counters include barriers/dispatch and overlap, so rearranging work could reduce elapsed waits without changing work, or lower phase time while leaving request wall unchanged.

The minimum useful next join is one clean, unchanged-route 32,768-actual-position prefill request with: (1) exact per-layer/chunk routed expert and M histogram, actual projection call count and work sum; (2) copy submit/complete IDs and exact payload bytes for that same request, with allocation type; (3) validated monotonic GPU event intervals plus separate host submit/wait spans, and phase dependency IDs; and (4) physical request wall/output count. Keep profiler-heavy runs out of the throughput control, reject invalid/backward timer pairs, and preserve unjoined events as unattributed. The timer-validity diagnostic report describes those guards; the in-progress root phase diagnostics should answer only what their valid events directly support.

For decode, first keep default-auto mode, actual input/output/MTP behavior, and source identity fixed; capture the same-request logical job bytes, exact row/call counts, GU/quant/Down and CPU-dispatch spans, per-worker busy/wait/tail distribution, and GPU overlap/dependency ledger. Compare physical wall and correctness before attributing a scheduler effect. If order changes bytes, MTP windows, or work coverage, classify it as a changed-work/architecture comparison. Only collect hardware counters or DRAM transactions if a named remaining question still needs them and the measurement is approved/available; the stream probe and blob accounting cannot substitute.
