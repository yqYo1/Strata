## Finding

Round53's synthetic oneMKL GEMM has the same relevant mathematical contract and row/column interpretation as the production non-batched DPCT GEMM: FP16 A and B, FP32 output and compute, transpose/non-transpose, alpha=1, beta=0, and matching dimensions and leading dimensions. Production descriptor defaults also resolve to oneMKL `compute_mode::unset` for this real-valued FP32-compute call; the direct probe omits the DPCT descriptor wrapper but does not select a conflicting mode. The differences are queue/timing-path and payload details, not a material semantic mismatch that invalidates the probe as a shape envelope. They do prevent calling it an exact production-call timing reproduction or a universal peak-TFLOP result.

The separate CPU evidence is an actual-weight, host-resident NT1 service calibration, not an instruction-throughput ceiling and not an inference/service bound. It closes one narrower question: measured GU, quantization, Down20 and complete component intervals for fixed synthetic activations with actual routed weights. NT2–4, live/default route distribution, end-to-end inference, and worker-busy accounting remain unmeasured here. The pool-fit gate is false for every cell, so pool cohort durations do not qualify a scheduling candidate.

## GEMM contract audit

Production call sites in `sycl/src/prefill/prefill.cpp` (GU at 4799, Down at 4802) call `m.gemm.f16` with each expert's row count `ne`. GU uses output width 1280 and K=N(hidden)=2560; Down uses output width N=2560 and K=640. Source-level `Gemm::f16` in `sycl/src/prefill/gemm.dp.cpp` dispatches column-major GEMM with A transposed, B not transposed, alpha 1, FP16 weight and activation buffers, FP32 result, compute type `f32`, `lda=K`, `ldb=K`, and `ldc=ldy` (the output row width). The call is ordinary single GEMM, not batched; there are no batch strides. The MoE call uses beta zero.

The hardware probe calls `oneapi::mkl::blas::column_major::gemm` with `(trans, nontrans, n, T, k, 1.0f, W, k, X, k, 0.0f, Y, n)`. Here W and X are FP16 and Y is FP32. This is exactly the same matrix layout conversion used by the row-major production buffers: transposed W represents the original row-major `N×K` weight; non-transposed X represents row-major `T×K` activation; column-major C represents row-major `T×N` output. Probe M=`n`, N=`T`, K=`k`, with the same leading dimensions. Its GU and Down shape families match the production dimensions across tested row counts.

The DPCT descriptor is initialized with `math_mode=mm_default` (`gemm.dp.cpp` descriptor setup). `dpct::blas::gemm` selects the half/half/float/float implementation (`blas_utils.hpp:1003–1008`) and obtains a compute mode from the descriptor and requested compute type. `deduce_compute_mode(f32, mm_default)` leaves mode `unset` for this real-valued call (the special complex branch does not apply; the TF32 branch does not apply). The direct oneMKL overload in the probe does not pass an explicit compute mode, so it likewise uses the library default/unset behavior. Intel documents the compute modes and GEMM overloads in its [oneMKL Compute Modes reference](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/onemkl/developer-reference-dpcpp/2024-0/compute-modes.html) and [oneMKL GEMM reference](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/onemkl/developer-reference-dpcpp/2025-0/gemm.html).

There are still timing and workload distinctions. Round53 uses an in-order SYCL queue with profiling disabled and host `steady_clock` around submit plus wait. Production passes its application queue through the DPCT descriptor; the descriptor wrapper and queue handling add a different host-side path. Queue profiling is a timing instrumentation setting, not an arithmetic mode, but enabling it can change observed overhead; the round53 number is not a profiled device-event duration. The probe's constant-pattern half payload (`±0.125` weights and ones activations) makes layout and output validation simple and exact, but does not represent production data-dependent latency or all numerical cases. This synthetic payload does not defeat the shape-envelope use: it narrows the claim to a shape-matched oneMKL operation under the recorded queue and data setup.

For scale context, round53's `hardware/validated-summary.json` contains seven samples per cell. In hot-weight mode, the reported effective rates include GU M=8 at 2.363 TFLOP/s and Down M=8 at 1.480 TFLOP/s; at M=8192 they are 52.787 TFLOP/s GU and 41.768 TFLOP/s Down. These are measured rates for those probe calls and data, not absolute hardware peaks. The M sweep is useful because the actual `ne` varies by expert and decode can be very small; one large-M rate cannot stand in for that distribution. Round53's CPU host-stream and device-transfer effective rates are separate evidence and are not memory-controller traffic counters.

## Native CPU evidence and limits

The closed `native-service-calibration-22-20-nt1-summary-v3` run uses actual IQ3_S routed expert blobs (selected cohort full blobs total 756,940,800 bytes; each cohort's selected GU and Down bytes exceed 64 MiB) resident in host memory, plus deterministic synthetic layer/token activations. It uses exact GU22/Down20/NT1, 25 measured rounds per train/holdout cohort and arm in each of four fresh processes, with six verified singleton CPUs on a Ryzen 5 5600X. Timing was explicitly scoped as a resident owned weight cohort beyond L3; it makes no DRAM or engine-latency claim. Input quantization, setup and correctness checks are outside service intervals. Fixed buffers, inputs, and pool job arrays are prepared before timed rounds; direct complete is one outer wall interval around GU, FF quantization and Down; pool phase timings are separate. A 192-job pool cohort time must not be divided by 192 and reported as per-job service.

Across the eight cells in the summary, train/holdout medians are approximately 0.195–0.203 ms per GU call, 0.000126–0.000152 ms per FFquant call, 0.0805–0.0846 ms per Down20 call, and 0.270–0.289 ms per direct-complete call. These intervals are genuine service measurements for that controlled NT1 cell, not isolated instruction costs: they include the production native row computation and its call-level machinery, while excluding input preparation and full inference scheduling. The 192-job pool median is about 14.8–17.5 ms across cells, but all R46 fit/holdout gates fail (the required interval-width criterion fails for every arm). Pool variance is also substantial. Therefore these batch durations are evidence about these observed pool runs only, not a qualified throughput estimate or task-count choice.

Correctness is materially stronger than a timing-only smoke. The closed NT1 receipts check independent byte-exact GGML GU and Down20 output, production activation/FF quantizer meaningful bytes, untouched guards, repeat equality, and actual pool partition/results. Fault cases cover wrong cohort count, header, format, overlapping IDs, and out-of-range IDs. The v3 summary says exact native/independent NT1 GU+Down20, quantizer, repeat and partition checks passed for each admitted timing process. This establishes NT1 implementation correctness for the sampled setup. It does not establish NT2–4 semantics or performance; the source README labels NT2 comparisons characterization rather than independent full-FFN qualification. NT2–4 timing, broader model-active worker residency, live route-conditioned weight/activation distributions, and a full inference run are absent.

The CPU instruction-versus-RAM split therefore remains open. A beyond-L3 owned working set reduces the chance that the whole cohort is L3-resident, but does not prove that each timed load reached DRAM, nor measure memory-controller transactions. A meaningful follow-up would pair the same pinned NT1/NT2–4 service cells with supported per-core retired-instruction/cycle and memory-controller read/write counters, while preserving the service receipts and checking counter multiplexing/coverage; compare measured bytes and cycles with the existing round53 host-stream calibration. The counter run would characterize this exact workload, not yield a universal CPU peak. Until those counters and default-workload worker-busy/route cohorts exist, neither a compute-ceiling nor a memory-bandwidth ceiling can be selected from these service medians alone.

## Evidence boundary and retained references

The findings above rely on the frozen source call sites and wrapper, round53 `README.md`, `root-review-corrections.json`, `hardware/validated-summary.json`, registry v76, and the v3 CPU summary plus per-process timing/correctness receipts and its pinned fixture/source records. Corrections from round53 remain in force: B570 runtime capacity is the recorded 10,666,115,072 bytes; the logged ~190 GB of expert-copy bytes are logical queued bytes, not physical DRAM transactions; earlier PTI H2D 6.163 GB/s was a mode-1/2K-input/1024-chunk result with roughly 30% profiler overhead, whereas the fresh host-USM median is about 6.449 GB/s. These facts do not change the GEMM contract audit, and none should be converted into an unmeasured DRAM or production-latency claim.
